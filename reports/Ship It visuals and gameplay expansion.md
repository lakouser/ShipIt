# Make the terminal grow, then deepen decisions

Ship It v0.4.2 is a well-built game whose screen and decision set both stop growing early, and the work should go in that order: fix a short list of bugs, make the terminal visibly grow with the company and point at the next goal, then spread out and deepen the decisions. The repository and the live artifact are the same build (byte-identical script and stylesheet, nine tabs, `db` and `user` capabilities), so every finding applies to what players see today ([visual audit](/home/user/ShipIt/research_notes/Ship%20It%20visuals%20and%20gameplay%20expansion/current_visuals_and_ui_audit.md)). The visual audit found a strong identity (a tmux session, shell-command panels, colour by meaning) and a smooth 60 fps, but also about fifteen fixable defects, feedback that is richest on the tap and nearly absent on purchases, rounds and major versions, and a late-game screen that differs from the first-launch screen only in its numbers. The gameplay audit found about twenty systems of which **eight of nine tabs open by minute 7.4**, then roughly 140 minutes with no new mechanic, nothing new after the first major version, and no goal or ending after the IPO ([gameplay audit](/home/user/ShipIt/research_notes/Ship%20It%20visuals%20and%20gameplay%20expansion/current_gameplay_systems_audit.md)). The genre research points the same way on both halves: the 2023–2026 hits are sold on a visible simulation and a finishable arc, and players quit at walls that bring no new decision. The recommendations below keep the terminal identity and reuse existing systems (workflows, forks, languages, challenges, the market and the line "only quarters to beat") instead of adding tabs. One caution applies throughout: only GitHub-hosted source code and MDN/W3C pages were read in full, every other web source was seen as a search extract, and the audits rest on bots, one seed, a headless browser and a patched late-game save.

## Fifteen defects to clear before any redesign

Nothing in the audit points to a structural problem: the game held 60.0 fps on phone and 59.5 on desktop under continuous tapping, with about 1,050 DOM nodes and a 233.7 kB script, and its reduced-motion support is thorough ([visual audit](/home/user/ShipIt/research_notes/Ship%20It%20visuals%20and%20gameplay%20expansion/current_visuals_and_ui_audit.md)). The defects are specific. Effort labels here and later are my estimates from the audits' descriptions of the code, not measurements: S is under a day, M is two to five days, L is one to three weeks.

The gameplay audit adds four defects of the same kind. The pacing bot picks the first "relaxed" sprint slot without checking its cooldown, the game refuses it, and the bot therefore merges about half the sprints available (946 against 1,874 in 720 minutes); with that one fix every milestone lands about 37% sooner and the first major arrives at 171 minutes, below the 180-minute floor the pacing test asserts ([tests/sim.ts:91-94](/home/user/ShipIt/tests/sim.ts#L91), [tests/pacing.test.ts:37-46](/home/user/ShipIt/tests/pacing.test.ts#L37)). `npm run simulate` does not print the report the README promises unless vitest is run with `--silent=false`. Stars, XP and banked stars are plain JavaScript numbers while lines are `Decimal`s, and an engaged bot held 1.2e33 stars at 12 hours, far past the 9e15 limit of exact integers; no failing case was found, but star spending and the "+75%" thresholds are exposed ([gameplay audit](/home/user/ShipIt/research_notes/Ship%20It%20visuals%20and%20gameplay%20expansion/current_gameplay_systems_audit.md)). Finally, `origin/main` is one commit behind the published build (v0.4.1 against v0.4.2), so new work should start from the v0.4.2 commit as the project notes require.

## The screen at the IPO looks like the screen at minute one

The audit's central visual finding is that a returning player shown two screenshots an hour apart could not tell which is later without reading numbers. The layout, the key, the readout and the palette are static from the first tap to the IPO; growth shows only as a file tab per package (two visible on phone), a small CI pane, up to four teammate name tags and a language switch. The complete art inventory is three pixel sprites, an ASCII duck, a boat and eight three-line faces ([visual audit](/home/user/ShipIt/research_notes/Ship%20It%20visuals%20and%20gameplay%20expansion/current_visuals_and_ui_audit.md)). The visual recommendations follow in priority order.

**1. Put the next goal on screen at all times (S to M).** Outside the ship tab the only sign that a release is worth shipping is a `!` after "ship", and nothing in the readout shows distance to the next star, round or major. The tmux bar and the readout are the natural home for a goal segment ("next ★ 66%", "release ready", "Series A 1.2K/2K"), replacing the self-normalised sparkline. Badges should carry counts and separate "new" from "needs you", since `!` currently means five different things. The precedents are in source code that was read in full: Antimatter Dimensions marks tabs once when a condition first becomes true and clears the mark on a visit ([tab-notifications.js](https://github.com/IvarK/AntimatterDimensionsSourceCode/blob/master/src/core/tab-notifications.js)), and The Modding Tree has a separate, subtler `prestigeNotify` highlight for "worth resetting" alongside a configurable glow colour for other notices ([layer-features.md](https://github.com/Acamaeda/The-Modding-Tree/blob/master/docs/layer-features.md)). On phone the same pass should make the tab bar fit without sideways scrolling; a tmux-style window list opened from the bar would stay in character. General mobile guidance favours three to five bottom destinations ([Smashing Magazine](https://smashingmagazine.com/2020/02/design-mobile-apps-one-hand-usage/)), and players of tab-heavy web incrementals say they cannot tell which tab needs attention ([galaxy.click comments](https://galaxy.click/comments/470?sort=top)).

**2. Move feedback to where the decisions are (M).** The tap gets a floating number, a count bump, typed code, a mode chip and a flow meter; the ship gets a well-staged 3.3-second splash with a pixel banner and a sailing boat. Buying a package, levelling a perk, hiring, a promotion and a package becoming affordable get a re-render and a log line, and the funding-round and major-version splashes are one static gold word with four `[ ok ]` lines. The audit's judgment is that feedback strength is inversely related to how much an action matters late in the game. The toolkit is small: the settings file of the "Juice it or lose it" demo groups it as tweening, stretch and squeeze, sounds, particles, shake, a freeze of up to 320 ms and "personality" ([Settings.as](https://github.com/grapefrukt/juicy-breakout/blob/master/src/com/grapefrukt/games/juicy/Settings.as)). For Ship It that means a squash on the buy button, a count-up on the rate that just changed, a one-shot highlight when a row becomes affordable, and round and major splashes built to the merge splash's standard (a ticker for the IPO, a rolling version banner for a major). Antimatter Dimensions stages its first reset as a 2-second full-screen animation with the state swap hidden at the midpoint, and gives every large animation its own toggle ([big-crunch.js](https://github.com/IvarK/AntimatterDimensionsSourceCode/blob/master/src/core/big-crunch.js), [player.js](https://github.com/IvarK/AntimatterDimensionsSourceCode/blob/master/src/core/player.js)); Ship It's reduce-motion switch already covers the comfort side. Sound deserves its own slice of this work. It is off by default and consists of keyboard clicks reused for every reward, and the vibration API is not implemented in WebKit, so on iPhones feel has to come from visuals and audio ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Navigator/vibrate), [W3C implementation report](https://w3c.github.io/vibration/reports/implementation.html)). Distinct short sounds for a ship, a star and an incident, plus a visible first-run offer to turn sound on, are cheap; one industry piece calls audio an often-overlooked, affordable way to sharpen feedback ([GameAnalytics](https://gameanalytics.com/?p=3400)). The counter-position should temper all of this: a GDC talk argues added polish costs immersion ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/video-indies-resist-the-urge-to-juice-it-or-lose-it-)), so shake should stay reserved for crits, bosses and resets.

**3. Make the workspace an honest, growing picture of the company (L, in slices).** This is the largest visual gain and the one best supported by the market evidence. The developer of (the) Gnorp Apologue set out to make "a more visual incremental game that was still an incremental game" and called visual incrementals the undersupplied category; the game sold 120,000 copies in its first month from about 1,700 launch wishlists, with discovery driven by YouTube ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/interview-the-gnorp-apologue), [GameDiscoverCo](https://newsletter.gamediscover.co/p/how-this-solo-dev-incremental-game)). The text-first tradition shows art is not required: A Dark Room ends with dozens of interdependent resources "using that same interface" it began with ([InformIT](https://informit.com/articles/article.aspx?p=2228802)), and Progressbar95 unlocks new OS versions as its progression ([Backloggd](https://backloggd.com/games/progressbar95/)). Ship It already has the right ideas at too small a size. The slices, cheapest first: let the key change with tap packages (label, keycap, a second row), since it is the most-touched object and never changes; let the tmux bar gain segments as systems unlock (users, chart rank, round); turn the package file tabs into a process or file-tree pane with count badges so all fifteen are visible; give the team a row of ASCII desks driven by the existing faces and roles; and draw the market as a chart instead of a table. The rule from the Gnorp press kit, visuals that "accurately portray" the numbers, is the constraint: every drawn element should be a function of something owned ([gnorp.dev](https://gnorp.dev/presskit/)).

**4. Shorten the panels (M).** On a phone the tool panel is a 332px window, and the TODO panel is 12.4 screens long because 93 achievements sit under the live tasks; ship stacks seven unrelated sections into 6.8 to 11.3 screens; rule paragraphs of five to eight lines scroll past permanently. The fixes are structural and on-theme: sub-sections inside ship (release, dotfiles, cap table, stats), achievements collapsed to unearned-first with a "hide completed" switch, rule text moved into the man pages that already exist with a `?` to open them, and two-line package rows. Antimatter Dimensions ships "hide completed achievement rows" and hideable tabs, and Kittens Game hides non-applicable buttons by default ([player.js](https://github.com/IvarK/AntimatterDimensionsSourceCode/blob/master/src/core/player.js), [settings.js](https://github.com/nuclear-unicorn/kittensgame/blob/master/js/settings.js)); a developer guide advises showing the next two or three upgrades, not all twenty ([dev.to](https://dev.to/aguier/i-built-7-idle-games-in-30-days-what-i-learned-about-incremental-design-5d3f)).

**5. Calm the first minute (S).** A new player currently starts with 200 lines, an achievement toast and two affordable packages before touching anything, and two tabs, a bug and a debt meter arrive inside thirty seconds; the goal of the game is first stated when the ship tab unlocks minutes later. Let the first tap and first purchase be earned, show the ship tab locked with its requirement from the start, and hold the debt box until debt costs something (it is free below 10). A Dark Room's developers cut the wait for the first surprise from 45 to about 15 seconds because they feared losing players before it ([design-process PDF](https://s3.us-east-2.amazonaws.com/amirrajan-public/design-process.pdf)), and one developer guide warns that showing everything in the first ten minutes makes new players leave ([Apptrove](https://apptrove.com/how-to-make-an-idle-game/)).

**6. Lower-priority visual work (S to M each).** Desktop is a rearranged phone layout capped at 1,240px, leaving 35% of a 1920px screen and 52% of a 2560px one empty; a second pane (pkgs beside ship, as a tmux split) is the obvious use, though the external evidence for it is thin. Phosphor and amber, the unlockable themes, collapse six hues into one and remove the game's main readability device; states should be double-coded with a glyph or weight, which is also what WCAG's use-of-colour guidance asks for ([W3C](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/use-of-color.html)). More editor palettes as unlock rewards would be cheap, in-character content: Bitburner ships 17 themes, mostly code-editor palettes ([Themes data](https://github.com/bitburner-official/bitburner-src/blob/dev/src/Themes/data/index.ts)). The welcome-back screen should report users, rivals and releases as well as lines, as Antimatter Dimensions does per resource ([away-progress-types.js](https://github.com/IvarK/AntimatterDimensionsSourceCode/blob/master/src/core/secret-formula/away-progress-types.js)), and end on the next action.

## Every decision is revealed by minute 35 and spent by the IPO

The gameplay audit's inventory is wide rather than deep. Packages, dotfiles, company perks, skills, titles and achievements are bought in a fixed order with no exclusion: the ten capped dotfiles cost 419 stars and are all owned by minute 215, the five rounds grant at least 40 equity against 37 needed, and every capped skill is owned at 41 XP. The real decisions are which project to ship, when to ship or cut a major, which fork to take and how to staff five roles, and all of them except challenges are on the table by about minute 35. After the IPO nothing unlocks; the gap between majors grows from about 20 to 80 minutes and the loop is "wait, cut major, buy 10x engineering" ([gameplay audit](/home/user/ShipIt/research_notes/Ship%20It%20visuals%20and%20gameplay%20expansion/current_gameplay_systems_audit.md)). The user's own Incremental Bible sets the test used below: "does it add a decision for the player, or only a bigger number?" ([Incremental Bible](https://claude.ai/artifact/4PkJgggzc3P4McXMubdh6X)).

**1. Fix the measuring stick first (S).** The README's pacing (first ship 7.4 minutes, Seed at 176, IPO at 350) describes a bot that never picks a fork, hires, fights a boss, answers mail, changes project or starts a challenge. A bot that does reaches the IPO in **102 minutes**, and single-change variants land between 170 and 278. Fix the sprint-cooldown bug, add an engaged bot to `tests/pacing.test.ts` with its own bounds, and report the "longest wait for something new" per phase, the check the Bible applies to the user's other games. Every later change touches `src/core/content.ts`, and its effect cannot be judged against a bot that ignores six systems.

**2. Re-space the reveals (S to M).** Sprints, TODO, mail, incidents, the team tab, the ship tab and forks all appear in the first eight minutes; then the roughly 140 minutes from the third ship to Seed add only package tiers and two workflows. Moving the team, choice mail, bosses and tech debt later, so each arrives at a wall, costs threshold changes and pacing-test runs. The evidence is qualitative but consistent: players in an r/incremental_games thread ask for "new mechanics being introduced on a slow pace" and complain of games where "after few hours you already know everything" ([thread mirror](https://reddit.zackpollard.pro/r/incremental_games/comments/1ko0i7y/what_makes_an_idleincremental_game_actually)), and a design guide puts it as "just as one layer's growth is mastered, a new one opens" ([bugnet.io](https://bugnet.io/blog/how-to-design-an-idle-or-incremental-game)). No source gave a numeric cadence. The gap itself needs one new mechanic, and the cheapest on-theme candidate is already drawn: the CI pane prints builds and flaky tests but has no rules. A test suite the player builds, which lowers escaped bugs and lifts the review score, would give releases a quality dimension; code review and tech debt exist as mechanics only in small or unreleased titles such as "Approve Please" and "Yet Another Incremental Game" ([itch.io](https://amllamojha.itch.io/approve-please), [Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/3729810)), so the space is open but unproven.

**3. Fix time away (S to M).** Offline pays 25% of automation for seven hours, computed as one lump from the rate at the moment of return; with `release.yml` on, a save can sit seconds after an auto-ship with almost nothing installed, so the same absence is worth a great deal or nearly nothing. Leaving the game open drives debt to 100, and users drain at 1% a minute after 45 quiet minutes. Use an average rate over the last release instead of the instantaneous one, raise the base rate, and soften or drop user churn. Pecorella's slides concede that offline limits "do pressure players to come back, but well-designed games without them have great retention anyway" ([Idle Chatter](https://pt.slideshare.net/AnthonyPecorella/idle-chatter-gdc-2016-59734260)); a Kittens Game bug report shows what extrapolating a momentary rate over a whole absence does ([issue 143](https://bitbucket.org/bloodrizer/kitten-game/issues/143/offline-progression-with-negative-catnip)); and a CHI study's follow-up describes idle games as rewarding the return ([NSF PAR](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10174274)). The daily streak deserves the same review: an EU consumer resource classes streak-based login bonuses as a pressure tactic ([Better Internet for Kids](https://better-internet-for-kids.europa.eu/adwiseonline/fomo)). No direct player evidence on churn or decay mechanics was captured, so this rests on the research framing and the Bible, not on sentiment data.

**4. Automate forks and remember the project (M).** Forks are the game's most frequent real choice (always taking the plugin or LTS option reached the IPO at 170 minutes against 212 for always taking the rewrite), but the engaged bot made **213,851 picks in 12 hours**, no workflow makes them, each open picker adds about 200px to the phone panel, and `release.yml` re-ships the current project while every reset silently returns it to web app. A per-package fork policy and a remembered project, issued as a new workflow file, turn a chore into a setting. The genre evidence is that automation should be the reward for mastering the thing automated: Antimatter Dimensions' autobuyers come from challenges and milestones ([TV Tropes](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/VideoGame/AntimatterDimensions)), players object to repeating a manual step "a dozen or so times" before automation arrives ([itch.io comment](https://itch.io/post/13778508)), and Increlution's developer made player input override automation and planned to cut its unlock from about 15 minutes to 5 ([Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1593350/discussions/0/4626981776940619365)).

**5. Make the language a real stack choice (M to L).** The editor switches from TypeScript to Rust, Go and Python by round with no rule attached, and no system anywhere has exclusive branches or limited slots; the only "build" is project plus team roles. A stack chosen at each round or major, with trade-offs (taps and crits, cheap automation, faster sprints, more tiers and more bugs), gives each run an identity. Cookie Clicker's Pantheon is the smallest structure found that yields builds: three slots, effects that differ by slot, and a swap budget that recharges slowly ([Pantheon](https://cookieclicker.wiki.gg/wiki/Pantheon)); Bitburner's BitNodes change the rules per run ([Bitburner docs](https://bitburner.readthedocs.io/en/latest/guidesandtips/recommendedbitnodeorder.html)). Two failures should shape the design. Realm Grinder's "combine everything" mercenaries left the plain factions weaker ([Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/610080/discussions/0/1736588252392812369)), and Gnorp players warn of "trap choices" that brick a run ([Vaporlens, auto-generated](https://vaporlens.app/app/1473350/the_gnorp_apologue)), so stacks need a preview and a free change at each reset. Ship It already has a dominant option to tune first: the CLI project reached Seed at 44 minutes against 176 for web app at four taps a second.

**6. Make challenges repeatable and pay them in automation (M).** The five challenge releases are the third layer's only new verb, and the engaged bot cleared four in 26 minutes and the fifth in the next major; they pay permanent multipliers and cannot be restarted. Record versions (fastest clear, with a capped bonus that scales) and automation rewards (the fork policy above, auto-started sprints late on, bug triage) would keep the verb alive after the IPO. Trimps advertises "over 50 different Challenges that tweak the Universe" ([Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1877960/Trimps)), and Realm Grinder's challenge perks apply whenever the related faction is played ([Realm Grinder wiki](https://realm-grinder.fandom.com/wiki/Research)). The same pass can name the stacking that already exists (coffee ×3 over turbo and sudo, each running effect adding debt) as a visible combo; Cookie Clicker keeps active play relevant with a Frenzy and Click Frenzy stack worth ×5,439 per click ([Cookie Clicker wiki](https://cookieclicker.fandom.com/wiki/Golden_Cookie?oldid=6162)).

**7. Give users a job on a medium clock (L).** Users affect nothing but chart rank, rank tops out at #1 of 16 (reached at 159 minutes by the engaged bot), and no system runs on a clock between about 12 minutes and a day. A queue that users fill over hours (issues, feature requests, support) and the player clears in a batch at each check-in would use the existing market, give an hourly reason to return and fill the widest unused part of the theme. Eric Guan's design caps different producers at 20 minutes, 5 hours and 2 days so several rhythms each have something to collect ([Idle Game Design Principles](https://ericguan.substack.com/p/idle-game-design-principles)); Cookie Clicker's garden runs on its own 3-to-5-minute ticks and pays in buffs the player times ([Garden](https://cookieclicker.wiki.gg/wiki/Garden)). The warnings are equally clear: Cookie Clicker's own wiki is quoted by players as calling its stock market not very profitable ([Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1454400/discussions/0/601897727418236566)), IdleOn players name growing daily chores as a reason to quit ([r/idleon mirror](https://reddit.birdcat.cafe/r/idleon/comments/1ctm2qi/lava_please_keep_idle_in_idleon)), and a game made only of support tickets "gets monotonous very quickly" ([GameWatcher](https://www.gamewatcher.com/tech-support-error-unknown/review)). The Bible's guide of side systems at 10 to 20% of income, with upkeep that automates on the main loop's schedule, fits.

**8. End the campaign at the IPO, then start the quarters (L).** The game's own text says "there are no more rounds to raise: only quarters to beat", and no mechanic called quarters exists; the story's last mail and man page arrive at Series A, a third of the way to the IPO. An ending screen (a run summary with time, releases and rank) followed by an optional endless mode of quarterly targets, with acquiring the fifteen rivals as the new verb, gives the late game a goal and a change of seat. Endings sell in this genre: Magic Research 2 advertises "a clear ending" at 60-plus hours ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/magic-research-2/id6478566840)), The Modding Tree has an endgame condition built in ([main-mod-info.md](https://github.com/Acamaeda/The-Modding-Tree/blob/master/docs/main-mod-info.md)), and the short premium Steam incrementals of 2024–2026, led by Nodebuster at 97% positive, sit at 91 to 99% ([Top Incremental Games](https://www.topincrementalgames.com/lists/incremental-games-with-skill-trees)). Universal Paperclips shows what a real shift looks like, with stages that "have very distinct play styles" ([Stages](https://universalpaperclips.fandom.com/wiki/Stages)), and The Founder shows the tone available: a board that demands growth forever, competitor buy-outs, and satire carried by the win condition ([Vice](https://www.vice.com/en/article/disrupt-the-industry-startup-simulator-game)). What players dislike is losing a save to replay ([Steam reviews of SPACEPLAN](https://store.steampowered.com/app/616110)), so the ending should never wipe anything.

**9. Leave rule scripting for last (L).** Growing `~/bin` into "when X, do Y" rules is the most on-theme idea and the most expensive. Antimatter Dimensions' Automator is a prize players share scripts for ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/automate-the-collapse-of-reality-in-clicker-antimatter-dimensions-now-on-steam)), but real-code games lose people at the manual ([Game Developer on TIS-100](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/zachtronics-i-shenzhen-i-o-i-is-a-game-for-people-who-code-games)). Toggles and thresholds on the existing workflows, extended one at a time, get most of the value. One thing not to add: the "keyboard mash, then juniors, then AI agents" arc and "vibe coding" jokes already headline several 2025–2026 Steam listings ([Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/3729810), [Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/3813330/Vibe_Clicker/)); that they are stale is my inference from saturation, not a measured player reaction.

The order puts measurement and certain fixes first, then changes that make existing play legible, then new content, with anything that changes the save format late, which is also how the Incremental Bible sequences additions for the user's other games ([Incremental Bible](https://claude.ai/artifact/4PkJgggzc3P4McXMubdh6X)).

The earlier prestige report's roadmap is almost entirely implemented in v0.4.2: cumulative stars with coefficient 2, the legible ship panel, the workflow ladder, the uncapped `.zshrc` sink, relative milestones and the semver major layer ([prestige report](/home/user/ShipIt/reports/Prestige%20systems%20in%20incremental%20games.md)). Its open items still stand and reappear above: a stack choice per major (item 5 here), challenge rewards that grant automation and repeatable challenges (item 6), a rule language for `~/bin` (item 9), a per-item keep list in the dotfiles tree, a highlighted or free first dotfile (not found in the code searched), and local logging of release cadence beyond the last ten releases. Four points differ from that report and should be stated plainly. Stars are now cumulative per run, since the last round or major, not over the lifetime as the report sketched; the spam-shipping test still passes, so its purpose is met. Its first-ship figure of 5.4 minutes is superseded by 7.4 minutes for the official bot, and the engaged bot ships at 3.9 minutes, under the five-minute floor the report treated as strict, because the floor is only tested against the official bot. The report proposed auto-starting sprints early in the automation ladder; the new simulations show sprints are the largest single income source (a bot that never sprints has 72 stars at minute 282, when the official bot raised Seed at 150 stars by minute 176), so I recommend holding sprint automation back as a late challenge reward. And the report warned that a second layer "earns its cost only if it adds decisions"; the major-version layer shipped, and the audit finds it added an endless multiplier whose only new verb, challenges, is finished within about two majors, which is why items 5 to 8 concentrate there.

## How far this evidence can be trusted

The external research is weaker than its citation count suggests. In all four web-research sessions the network allowlist blocked page fetches and the search budget ran out before the last batch of queries. Only GitHub-hosted material was read in full: the source of Antimatter Dimensions, Kittens Game, A Dark Room, The Modding Tree, Bitburner's themes and the Juicy Breakout demo, plus MDN and W3C pages read through their source repositories. Everything else, including every sales figure, developer quote, review and forum comment, is a search-result extract; wording is close paraphrase, and attribution to a specific page within a result set is sometimes approximate ([visual design notes](/home/user/ShipIt/research_notes/Ship%20It%20visuals%20and%20gameplay%20expansion/incremental_visual_design_and_juice.md), [expansion mechanics notes](/home/user/ShipIt/research_notes/Ship%20It%20visuals%20and%20gameplay%20expansion/incremental_expansion_mechanics.md), [themed games notes](/home/user/ShipIt/research_notes/Ship%20It%20visuals%20and%20gameplay%20expansion/coding_and_startup_themed_games.md), [market notes](/home/user/ShipIt/research_notes/Ship%20It%20visuals%20and%20gameplay%20expansion/incremental_market_retention_and_sentiment.md)). Several per-game complaint summaries come from machine-generated digests of Steam reviews and are indicative only. Third-party sales trackers are review-count models that disagree by a factor of two, so this report quotes developer-stated sales where it quotes any. The main community, r/incremental_games, was reachable only as a few mirrored snippets. No source gave a numeric cadence for new mechanics, no retention or A/B data was found for any UI practice, and no player evidence was captured on streak pressure, churn-while-away or UI clutter as quit reasons. Claims that a theme is unclaimed mean "not found in the searches run".

The audits have their own limits. Every pacing number comes from deterministic bots on one seed with no time away, so the offline conclusions are derived from formulas, not measured, and no human has been observed. The visual audit ran in headless Chromium on Linux: no real phone, no Claude app viewer, no audio heard, and symbol glyphs drawn with this machine's fallback font. The late game was a hand-patched save with every fork picker open, which inflates the pkgs panel length. The live artifact was read and hashed, not run. The Incremental Bible was read except for its sources lists and two late sections of its second tab. The screenshots and simulation outputs behind the audits sit in a session scratch directory that can be cleared; the numbers used here are copied into the notes.

## Conclusion

The two audits describe one problem from two sides. Ship It reveals almost everything, to the eye and to the hand, in its first half hour, and then asks the player to watch numbers for five hours. The genre evidence, thin as some of it is, agrees with the user's own design document on the remedy: growth has to be seen, and each wall has to bring a decision. Ship It is unusually well placed for both, because its fiction already contains the missing pieces. A terminal can gain panes, processes and status segments without a single illustration; the cosmetic language swap, the idle users, the rule-less CI pane and the sentence about quarters are each a system waiting to be switched on.

The cheapest high-value step is the least glamorous: a bot that plays the way an engaged player does. The earlier report found the harness measuring a strategy no informed player would use, and this one finds the same blind spot in a new form, a README that describes a 350-minute road to the IPO which an engaged player covers in 102. Until the pacing tests see forks, projects, hires and sprints, every content change proposed here is tuned against the wrong game. After that, the first real test of these recommendations is a person on a real phone, with sound on, playing through the first ship.
