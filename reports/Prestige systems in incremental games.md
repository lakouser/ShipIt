# Make Ship It releases worth waiting for

Ship It's prestige loop fails for a structural reason, not a tuning one: stars come from a cube root of the lines written in the *current* release and each adds an uncapped +10% to output, so splitting one release into k smaller ones yields k^(2/3) times the stars and the one-star reset gets cheaper with every release. Measured with the repo's own bot, **shipping on a fixed one-minute timer earns 7.0 million stars in 24 hours against 61,696 for the "wait for +75%" rule the pacing test assumes, 114 times more**, while all ten dotfiles max out at 419 stars by hour 2.9 and the last producer is reached within a minute of every run from about hour 4. The highest-leverage fix is the one Cookie Clicker and AdVenture Capitalist use: compute stars from *cumulative* shipped lines. In a scratch copy of the game run for this report, `floor(2·cbrt(totalShipped / 2M))` kept the tested pacing within 5% (58,460 stars at 24 h), passed `tests/pacing.test.ts` at 1 and 4 taps/s, doubled the first release's payoff to 2 stars, and turned ship-at-every-star spam from the best strategy into a 36× loss, leaving a broad optimum where shipping at +75%, every 30 minutes or every hour all land within 4% of each other. That fix has to come first, because everything else the genre recommends (auto-ship, autobuyers, a second "major version" layer) amplifies whatever the base formula rewards. After it, the priorities are making the ship decision legible with stars per minute and a release log, filling the post-three-hour void with automation and an uncapped star sink, and only then a semver-themed second layer whose job is to add new verbs such as stack choices and challenge releases rather than another multiplier. All of it fits the codebase as long as new state goes through a `SAVE_VERSION` 3 migration and no layer ever resets `lifetime`.

## Shipping every minute beats the tested strategy 114 to 1

Today a ship converts `floor(cbrt(thisRelease / 2,000,000))` stars ([src/core/ship.ts:12](/home/user/ShipIt/src/core/ship.ts#L12)), wipes lines, packages, the active sprint and the backlog, and keeps stars, dotfiles, achievements, TODO quest bonuses, `~/bin` scripts and running timed effects ([src/core/ship.ts:32-48](/home/user/ShipIt/src/core/ship.ts#L32)). Each star ever earned adds 0.1 to a term that multiplies all output ([src/core/economy.ts:20](/home/user/ShipIt/src/core/economy.ts#L20)), and stars also buy ten capped perks costing **419 stars in total** without lowering that bonus ([Ship It measurements](/home/user/ShipIt/research_notes/Prestige%20systems%20in%20incremental%20games/ship_it_current_system.md)). The pacing harness assumes players ship once available stars reach 75% of their current total because "players usually wait for a meaningful jump" ([tests/sim.ts:87](/home/user/ShipIt/tests/sim.ts#L87)). The game rewards the opposite.

The mechanism is simple once Ship It is placed in its genre family. Kongregate's Anthony Pecorella sorts prestige formulas by their input: lifetime earnings (Cookie Clicker, AdVenture Capitalist), earnings since the last reset (Egg, Inc.) or purchases since the last reset (Clicker Heroes) ([The Math of Idle Games, Part III](https://www.kongregate.com/en/pages/the-math-of-idle-games-part-iii)). Cookie Clicker's prestige level is `floor(cbrt(all-time cookies / 1e12))` ([Cookie Clicker main.js](https://github.com/ozh/cookieclicker/blob/gh-pages/main.js#L4185)), the same cube root as Ship It applied to a running total, so repeating an identical run pays less each time. Ship It's per-run version pays the same forever and keeps getting cheaper: the star multiplier applies from the first second, late runs reach their first star in **1–2 seconds**, a ship resets every standup cooldown, timed effects persist and `bootstrap.sh` hands out a free doubling. Because the cube root is concave, cutting the same lines into 100 releases yields 21.5 times the stars, and in-run growth cannot compete: once the producer ladder is climbed, each 25-copy doubling costs ×32.9 in lines, an output elasticity of about 0.2, far below the t³ growth that would make waiting pay under a cube root ([Ship It measurements](/home/user/ShipIt/research_notes/Prestige%20systems%20in%20incremental%20games/ship_it_current_system.md)).

The harness hides all of this. The README's pacing claims ([README.md:44](/home/user/ShipIt/README.md#L44)) and the 120-minute test describe the patient bot, which an informed min-maxer outruns by two orders of magnitude within a day. The same blind spot leaves smaller problems in place: `npm run simulate` defaults to 240 minutes and fails its own end-of-run top-producer assertion because the bot owns a quantum compiler by minute 168, and the display prints raw values such as "7004030 stars" and `outputBonus = 700403.00` that were never designed for real star counts ([src/ui/header.ts:49](/home/user/ShipIt/src/ui/header.ts#L49), [src/ui/panels/ship.ts:11](/home/user/ShipIt/src/ui/panels/ship.ts#L11)).

## Counting all shipped code restores the reason to wait

Cumulative formulas lengthen runs on their own, which is why the genre's biggest games use them. Pecorella notes that under such a formula "if you reset at the same point you will earn literally no more prestige currency" ([Game Developer, Part III](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/the-math-of-idle-games-part-iii)). AdVenture Capitalist pays angels as roughly 150·√(lifetime/1e15), a figure available only through search summaries of that article, and players settle into his rule of thumb of resetting at about **+50% to +200%** of current prestige currency ([Quest for Progress, GDC Europe 2016](https://media.gdcvault.com/gdceurope2016/presentations/Pecorella_Anthony_Quest%20for%20Progress.pdf)). The alternative is a hard gate, with precedents in Antimatter Dimensions' crunch autobuyer, which can reset only when the gain reaches X times the best previous gain ([big-crunch-autobuyer.js](https://github.com/IvarK/AntimatterDimensionsSourceCode/blob/master/src/core/autobuyers/big-crunch-autobuyer.js#L110)), and in Kittens Game, which pays paragon only for kittens above 70 ([game.js](https://github.com/nuclear-unicorn/kittensgame/blob/master/game.js#L5547)).

To test both ideas against Ship It's actual economy, I patched `ship.ts` in a scratch copy of `src/` (no tracked file changed) and reran the repo's instrumented bot for 24 simulated hours (seed 7) and the real `tests/pacing.test.ts`. The cumulative variant stores total shipped lines and pays the increase in `floor(c·cbrt(total / 2M))`, still requiring 2M lines in the current release before any ship. The gate variant keeps today's formula but refuses a ship worth less than 75% of the stars already held. Outputs are in `/tmp/claude-0/-home-user-ShipIt/2f3cf838-2fba-5b0e-b5b4-598383e07f78/scratchpad/exp/`.

The cumulative curve with c = 2 keeps today's tested experience almost exactly and passes the pacing test at both 1 and 4 taps/s. What changes is the incentive: shipping at every star now earns **36 times fewer** stars than patience, five-minute cadences lose by 2.3×, and shipping at +75%, every 30 minutes or every hour all finish within 4% of one another, with a full-doubling wait costing 11%. That broad, flat optimum is the real design target. It lets players ship when it suits them, which practitioner guides frame as the difference between a choice and a reset that feels "compulsory, confusing, or too costly" ([Droplet Idle](https://dropletidle.com/pages/blog/guide-to-idle-game-prestige-mechanics/)), and it makes the harness's +75% bot a fair stand-in for real optimal play. The gate removes the exploit too and is pacing-identical by construction, since the bot already obeys that rule; it needs no save change and breaks no unit test, because both ship tests start from zero stars. Its optimum is a knife edge, though: a player shipping on a 30-minute floor ends up 25% behind, and a "needs N more lines" wall replaces a soft curve. It is the right stopgap and the wrong end state.

Implementation is small, and moving the cube root onto `Decimal` also removes the latent bug where `toNumber()` before `cbrt` would turn lines past 1.8e308 into Infinity stars that `int()` loads back as 0:

```ts
// GameState gains `shipped: Decimal`: total lines ever shipped, monotonic, never reset by a release.
const C = 2;
const total = (lines: Decimal) => Math.floor(C * lines.div(SHIP.linesPerStar).pow(1 / 3).toNumber() + 1e-9);
export function starsAvailable(s: GameState): number {
  if (s.thisRelease.lt(SHIP.linesPerStar)) return 0; // keeps the 2M-line first ship (5–35 min bound)
  return total(s.shipped.add(s.thisRelease)) - total(s.shipped);
}
// ship(): s.shipped = s.shipped.add(s.thisRelease) before zeroing thisRelease.
// v2 → v3 migration: shipped = 2M · (stars / C)^3, so total(shipped) === stars and no one loses a star.
```

The migration follows the War Clicks team's practice of carrying over everything players had earned and spent "so players wouldn't feel cheated" ([Game Developer postmortem](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/from-a-prototype-to-one-of-the-biggest-idle-games-in-3-years-postmortem)): a save that already spam-shipped to millions of stars keeps them and simply earns few new ones until its releases catch up. The change bumps `SAVE_VERSION` to 3 with one parse line for `shipped` in `deserialize` ([src/core/save.ts:19](/home/user/ShipIt/src/core/save.ts#L19)), alters the two ship unit tests (16M lines from zero now pays 4 stars and per-tap output becomes 1.4) ([tests/game.test.ts:296-324](/home/user/ShipIt/tests/game.test.ts#L296)), and requires `linesForStars` to invert the new curve for the "next ★ at" bar. Cloud arbitration is untouched because it compares `lifetime`, which never resets ([src/platform/saves.ts:210](/home/user/ShipIt/src/platform/saves.ts#L210)). The coefficient is a dial: with c = 1, stars at 24 h fall to 14,765 and perks max at 5.5 hours, a slower game if that is wanted. These results come from a deterministic bot with one seed that buys greedily on base output and never goes offline, so absolute minutes will differ for humans, but gaps of 36–114× dwarf any plausible seed or play-style noise.

## After three hours there is nothing left to buy or reach

Fixing the formula does not fix the late game. In the cumulative run the quantum compiler is bought within 15 seconds of every release from release 16 on, and run lengths still stretch from 88 to 402 minutes. In today's game three signals arrive together between hours 2.5 and 5: perks maxed, top producer reached and runs lengthening about 1.4× per release. After that every hook goes flat: **unspent stars reach 188,548 by hour 42**, a marginal star is worth 0.1% of output at 1,000 stars, milestone stars made up 0.11% of all stars over a day because their quadratic line targets are trivially passed, release and star achievements end at v10 and 50 stars by about hour 2.2, and 1 and 4 taps/s converge from release 12 so tapping stops mattering ([Ship It measurements](/home/user/ShipIt/research_notes/Prestige%20systems%20in%20incremental%20games/ship_it_current_system.md)).

The genre's first answer is automation handed out as the reward for mastering a layer. Antimatter Dimensions' Eternity milestones are front-loaded: an autobuyer at 1 Eternity, a start with all challenges and autobuyers at 2, and the layer's own autobuyer at 100 ([eternity-milestones.js](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/IvarK/AntimatterDimensionsSourceCode/master/src/core/secret-formula/eternity/eternity-milestones.js)). One reviewer sums up the principle as "every long-tail incremental eventually automates its own early game so attention can move to the frontier" ([playwanderer, Synergism review](https://playwanderer.online/game-reviews/synergism)). The complaints mark the boundaries: NGU Idle reviewers resent automation gatekept behind cooldowns ([Steam reviews](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1147690/negativereviews/?browsefilter=toprated)), an Idle Spiral player dismissed auto-prestige that was "only good once, maybe twice" ([Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1827980/discussions/0/3883851232913830967)), and Revolution Idle's community calls permanent automation "the crucial QoL feature of the game" after an earlier layer wiped it ([revolutionidle.com](https://revolutionidle.com/guide/tips-tricks)). For Ship It that means a release-count ladder front-loaded at v2, v3, v5, v10 and v25 (auto-buy tier-1 packages, auto-start relaxed sprints, auto-triage bugs), ending in a `.github/workflows/release.yml` auto-ship that triggers when pending stars reach X% of held stars. Under the cumulative formula a +75% setting sits within 4% of the best policy measured, so the automation is near-optimal and safe; under today's formula it would automate the 114× exploit.

The second answer is a sink that never runs dry. Kittens Game and The Modding Tree tame runaway currencies with asymptotic caps and power softcaps ([Kittens game.js](https://github.com/nuclear-unicorn/kittensgame/blob/master/game.js#L2726), [Modding Tree game.js](https://github.com/Acamaeda/The-Modding-Tree/blob/master/js/game.js#L27)); Ship It's opposite problem is that nothing can be bought after 419 stars. An uncapped perk such as `.zshrc` at ×1.05 output per level, with a cost that doubles each level, grows its level count with the base-2 log of stars, so its total effect rises only like stars^0.07 and cannot run away. It pairs naturally with milestones that target the previous release ("beat v12's lines", "merge N sprints") instead of 16M·r², achievements past v10 and 50 stars, and a career-title ladder keyed to lifetime stars, an official version of the "Kilofarmer/Megafarmer" ranks Egg, Inc. players invented for themselves ([Egg Inc wiki](https://egg-inc.fandom.com/wiki/Earnings_Bonus)).

The third answer is a ship decision players can read. Antimatter Dimensions prints "Current: X IP/min" and "Peak: Y IP/min" on the crunch button ([BigCrunchButton.vue](https://github.com/IvarK/AntimatterDimensionsSourceCode/blob/master/src/components/ui-modes/prestige-header/BigCrunchButton.vue#L84)) and keeps a last-10-runs table of time, gain and gain per minute ([AD wiki](https://antimatter-dimensions.fandom.com/wiki/Statistics)) that players use to judge whether a strategy helped ([Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1399720/discussions/0/3770109979122558558)). Ship It's GitHub-PR diff preview is a good base; it should show the effect as a relative change ("+N★ → ×1.18 output") rather than a raw stars×0.1 sum, stars per minute against this release's best, a hint once pending stars hit +50% of held, and a `git log --oneline` release history.

The fourth answer is a first release that clearly pays. Practitioner guides agree "the first reset is the hardest one to take" and a reset "only works if it clearly pays off" ([Onirealms](https://onirealms.com/guides/how-to-design-an-idle-game/)), that the next run should feel faster within about 30 seconds ([DEV Community](https://dev.to/aguier/i-built-7-idle-games-in-30-days-what-i-learned-about-incremental-design-5d3f)), and AdVenture Communist pays a capsule and a first researcher at the moment of reset ([AdCom wiki](https://adventurecommunist.fandom.com/wiki/Motherland_Walkthrough)). Ship It's first ship pays one star (+10%) and one perk, well short of that bar. The cumulative formula doubles it to two stars, and a highlighted or free first dotfile would add a visible change; the ship must not move earlier, since it lands at 5.4 minutes against a strict 5-minute floor. One existing choice should stay exactly as it is: stars keep their bonus after being spent. AdVenture Capitalist's spend-and-lose-the-bonus angels spawned threads titled "Angel Sacrifice stupid?" ([Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/346900/discussions/0/487870763303586413/)) and needed 1% warning prompts, while Cookie Clicker separates prestige level from spendable chips ([main.js](https://github.com/ozh/cookieclicker/blob/gh-pages/main.js#L16959)). Any new sink should spend stars without touching the +10% they grant.

## A second layer should add a verb, not a multiplier

Deep incrementals earn their reputation when each layer changes how the game plays. Antimatter Dimensions' layers brought new producer chains, a respec-able study tree with presets, randomized glyph equipment and finally an Automator scripting language ([AD how-to-play source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/IvarK/AntimatterDimensionsSourceCode/master/src/core/secret-formula/h2p.js)), and its creator singles out Reality for "plenty of choices to make and variance between player experiences" ([IncrementalDB interview](https://www.incrementaldb.com/community/interview/31)). Pecorella listed "prestige variation", changing the rules when prestiging, as a genre innovation ([Idle Chatter, GDC 2016](https://www.slideshare.net/slideshow/idle-chatter-gdc-2016-59734260/59734260)). The failures are equally instructive: Synergism is reviewed as "a huge reset ladder that doesn't always reinvent itself" ([playwanderer](https://playwanderer.online/game-reviews/synergism)), Realm Grinder's deeper reset makes "every Reincarnation a new Early Game Hell" ([TV Tropes](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/VideoGame/RealmGrinder)), and NGU Idle's hardest mode drew "nothing happens in Sadistic mode. You're just waiting around" ([Metacritic](https://www.metacritic.com/game/ngu-idle/user-reviews/)). Some players dislike resets outright, and Melvor Idle's developer is "not in favour of prestige systems" ([Melvor wiki](https://wiki.melvoridle.com/w/FAQ)), so any second layer should be optional and should arrive only after the first is fixed.

Ship It's theme hands it the layer: semantic versioning. Every release is currently shown as `v{n}.0` and the minor digit is never used ([Ship It measurements](/home/user/ShipIt/research_notes/Prestige%20systems%20in%20incremental%20games/ship_it_current_system.md)). Shipping would become a minor release (v3.4), and a **major version** (v4.0) would become the second reset, wiping stars and dotfiles. It should unlock after roughly 10–20 releases and be teased by a locked tab stating its requirement, the way Antimatter Dimensions teases Eternity. That spacing fits the genre norm of dozens of lower resets per higher one; Realm Grinder places its Ascensions at the end of R39, R99, R159 and R219 ([NamuWiki](https://en.namu.wiki/w/Realm%20Grinder)).

What a major version adds should be decisions. The first is a stack choice per major in the spirit of Realm Grinder's per-run factions ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/realm-grinder-guide/)), where each stack reshapes the ladder: `rust` favouring taps and crits at the expense of automation, `python` making automation cheap, `go` speeding sprints, `js` offering many cheap tiers with twice the bugs. The second is a set of optional "challenge releases" behind feature flags, following Antimatter Dimensions' cleanest pattern, in which each restricted run pays the autobuyer for the thing it restricted ([normal-challenges.js](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/IvarK/AntimatterDimensionsSourceCode/master/src/core/secret-formula/challenges/normal-challenges.js)), and Kittens Game's trick for repeatability, a capped "inactive" bonus that scales with completions ([challenges.js](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/nuclear-unicorn/kittensgame/master/js/challenges.js)).

| Challenge release | Restriction | Reward |
|---|---|---|
| Legacy codebase | Steeper package price growth | Package auto-buy |
| No QA | Three times the bugs | Bug auto-triage |
| Solo dev | Only six producer tiers | Start with tiers unlocked |
| Hotfix Friday | Constant incidents | Incident auto-resolve |
| Clean install | No dotfiles, like Cookie Clicker's "Born Again" mode ([wiki](https://cookieclicker.wiki.gg/wiki/Challenge_Mode)) | A terminal theme |

The deepest optional reward would grow the `~/bin` scripts into a tiny rule language; Antimatter Dimensions' Automator, which ships a block mode for non-programmers, shows players treat programming the game as a prize. It is the most on-theme idea here and also the most expensive. Two rules keep the layer clear of the genre's mistakes. The keep list should be explicit, as in Synergism's colour-coding of upgrades that reset on Ascension, on Singularity or never ([IncrementalDB](https://www.incrementaldb.com/update/29319880-ffc0-49fb-9a54-840f44b618bd)), which Ship It's dotfiles tree could copy as "kept on release / kept on major / permanent". And earned automation should never be taken back, with each major starting from a kit so the new layer is not early-game hell. The codebase adds two hard constraints. Cloud saves keep whichever copy has more `lifetime` and demote the other to a backup ([src/platform/saves.ts:228](/home/user/ShipIt/src/platform/saves.ts#L228)), so a major version may reset `shipped`, stars and perks but never `lifetime`, and it should leave the `release` counter monotonic and derive `v{major}.{minor}` only for display. New perks also need ids in `PERKS`, because unknown ids are dropped on load, and every new field needs a parse line plus a version bump ([README.md:86](/home/user/ShipIt/README.md#L86)).

## A prioritized roadmap that fits the codebase

| # | Change | Evidence | Codebase cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Test the policy players will actually use: add a spam-policy check to `tests/pacing.test.ts` (a 5-minute cadence must not beat the +75% bot), evaluate the top-producer rule at minute 120 from the timeline rather than at run end, and fix the README pacing line | 114× gap between policies; `npm run simulate` fails its own assertion | Tests and docs only |
| 2 | Cumulative shipped-lines stars with c ≈ 2, using the 75% gate as a no-migration stopgap if needed | Cookie Clicker and AdCap formulas; scratch sim: 58,460 vs 61,696 stars, spam 36× worse, pacing test passes | `SAVE_VERSION` 3 with a migration seeding `shipped`; two unit tests; `linesForStars` inverse |
| 3 | Legible ship panel: `fmt()` for stars and bonus, the "×" effect, stars per minute now vs. best, a +50% hint, a release log | AD rate readout and last-10 table; Cookie Clicker's live gain counter; Pecorella's rule | UI; the release log rides the v3 bump |
| 4 | Bigger, not earlier, first release: 2 stars from step 2 plus a highlighted first dotfile | First-reset guidance; AdCom's capsule; 0.4 min of slack on the 5-minute floor | Content and UI |
| 5 | Release-count automation ladder ending in an auto-ship workflow with a "+X% stars" trigger | AD milestones and autobuyers; NGU and Idle Spiral complaints | New `PERKS` ids or milestone rewards; only after step 2 |
| 6 | Uncapped doubling-cost star sink, milestones relative to the last release, achievements past v10 and 50★, career titles | 188,548 idle stars; milestones at 0.11% of stars | New `PERKS` id; milestone parsing accepts only `lines`, `owned` and `sprints` task kinds today |
| 7 | Optional major-version layer with stack choice and challenge releases | AD, Realm Grinder, Pecorella's prestige variation, and the critiques above | Another save bump; never reset `lifetime` or `release` |

The order follows dependencies rather than effort. Step 1 costs nothing and is the only way to prove later steps work, because the current harness measures a strategy no informed player uses. Steps 2 through 4 make the core loop honest, turning the ship button into a real decision with a readable payoff, and step 2 is the one change that measurably fixes the dominant strategy. Steps 5 and 6 fill the void after hour three, and they are safe only once step 2 lands, because automation and multiplicative sinks scale whatever the formula rewards. Step 7 is the most expensive, and the evidence says a new layer earns its cost only if it adds decisions; if the first six steps keep players engaged, it may not be needed at all.

## Conclusion

The central lesson is about sequence. The genre's most praised tools (automation, sinks, new layers) each multiply the incentives of the base formula, so bolted onto a per-run cube root, auto-ship would turn a 114× exploit into an idle default and a second layer would compound it. The first redesign task is deciding what a release is worth; everything else is built on that answer. The simulation also reframes the goal: the aim is not to pin players to one ideal cadence but a broad plateau where shipping at +75%, half-hourly or hourly all pay about the same, which honors the community's demand that prestige be a choice rather than a chore and, as a side effect, makes the pacing harness's patient bot truthful.

Ship It also starts with an unusual advantage. Other games spend effort justifying resets with fiction, from Cookie Clicker's "Ascend" to a devlog that renamed its reset "Reboot the Network" ([DEV Community](https://dev.to/tanishpaul1106/aura-data-and-prestige-designing-the-economy-of-doomscroll-2077-293f)); shipping a release is already an achievement, and semver already gives the game a second digit to grow into. What remains uncertain is human behaviour. Every figure here comes from bots with one seed and no offline play, much of the genre's player-sentiment evidence was read through search summaries and should be treated as indicative, and logging time-to-first-release and release cadence locally would be the cheapest way to check these recommendations against real players.
