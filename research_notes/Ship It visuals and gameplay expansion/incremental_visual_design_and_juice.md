## 1. Feedback and juice: what click/tap feedback do well-regarded incrementals use, and what do the foundational "juice" talks say?

### Takeaway
The canonical juice toolkit is small and well documented: tween everything in, squash/stretch on impact, layered sound, particles, brief decaying shake, a few-millisecond freeze, and "personality"; all of it is feedback layered on unchanged rules. For idle games the sourced advice is to spend it on the click, the purchase and the milestone, to scale it with the importance of the event, and to keep it switchable, because genre veterans turn effects off and critics note that juice can cost immersion and legibility.

### Cited Findings
- **"Juice it or lose it" (Martin Jonasson and Petri Purho, 2012).** The talk takes a dull Breakout clone and adds effects live "without changing the rules underneath"; summaries describe it as a beat-by-beat walkthrough using "tweens, scaling, screenshake, and more", with the thesis that the "juicier" a game is, the more fun it is to play ("little details, little moments of surprise and delight"). [extract] — [roblog.co.uk summary, 2024](https://roblog.co.uk/2024/03/juicy-games/); [maldorne.org summary, 2022](https://maldorne.org/2022/04/08/game-design-juice-it-or-lose-it/)
- Date/venue conflict: one extract places the talk in May 2012 at Nordic Game Jam, another at GDC Europe 2012. Treat as "2012, given more than once". [extract] — [dev.to "The Art of Screen Shake"](https://dev.to/dat_one_dev/the-art-of-screen-shake-33bi); [roblog.co.uk](https://roblog.co.uk/2024/03/juicy-games/)
- **The actual effect list from the talk's demo source code [read].** The settings file of the open-sourced demo groups the toggles under these headers, in this order: *Colors*; *Tweening* (tween-in of y / rotation / scale, with delay, a default duration of 0.7 s and a selectable easing equation); *Stretch and squeeze* (paddle stretch, ball extra scale, ball rotate, ball stretch, ball glow, block "jelly", bouncy wall lines); *Sounds* (separate wall, block and paddle sounds, plus music); *Particles* (ball collision particles, block destruction with scale / gravity / push / rotate / darken / shatter, paddle-collision particles, ball trail of length 30); *Screen shake* (on/off plus a power value, default 0.5); *Freeze/Sleep* (freeze duration slider 0–320 ms, speed multiplier, fade-in and fade-out 0–160 ms); *Personality* (paddle face, eyes that look at the ball, smile); *Finish him* (screen colour glitch). — [grapefrukt/juicy-breakout Settings.as](https://github.com/grapefrukt/juicy-breakout/blob/master/src/com/grapefrukt/games/juicy/Settings.as)
- **"The art of screenshake" (Jan Willem Nijman, Vlambeer, November 2013, INDIGO Classes / Control Conference, Amsterdam).** He shows a dull side-scrolling shooter and applies 30 small tweaks that each make it slightly better; examples in the write-up include shaking the screen slightly in the direction opposite to the player's gunfire and giving enemies a 33% chance to explode harmlessly on death. [extract] — [Game Developer: "Vlambeer co-founder shares advice on building better action games"](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/vlambeer-co-founder-shares-advice-on-building-better-action-games)
- Items from that talk confirmed by secondary outlines: hit pause, permanence (debris/smoke that stays), and bigger bullets. [extract] — [DK Liao recreation devlog](https://dkliao.itch.io/the-art-of-screenshake-recreation/devlog/451576/quick-breakdown-of-all-the-effects); [Univ. Bayreuth "Game Feel" lecture PDF](https://medienwissenschaft.uni-bayreuth.de/wp-content/uploads/GxD-10-Game-Feel.pdf)
- Third-party tuning rules of thumb (a guide, not the talk): keep shake brief, roughly 50–150 ms; fade intensity out rather than cutting it; scale shake to the importance of the action; make hit pauses longer for heavier impacts. [extract, low-authority source] — [gamedo-live juice guide](https://gamedo-live.pages.dev/news/game-feel-juice-guide-indie-devs)
- Counter-positions: Folmer Kelly's GDC talk "Don't Juice It or Lose It" argues added polish makes a game feel more alive "but we're actually losing a level of immersion"; a ModDB dev post argues screenshake is no fix for design problems and no single effect is a catch-all. [extract] — [Game Developer video write-up](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/video-indies-resist-the-urge-to-juice-it-or-lose-it-); [ModDB "Effects are excellent"](https://www.moddb.com/games/engauge/news/effects-are-excellent)
- GameAnalytics' juice article frames juicing as taking a game that already works and adding layers of satisfaction, and says audio is an often-overlooked, affordable way to sharpen feedback for low-budget teams. [extract] — [GameAnalytics](https://gameanalytics.com/?p=3400)
- **Cookie Clicker:** an analysis notes that nearly every achievement, cookie, generator or upgrade comes with visual feedback, and that the game rarely takes cookies away except on purchase. Golden cookies are described as sudden momentum spikes that break the steady climb; at their fastest they appear 1.25–3.5 minutes apart. [extract; first item is from either the Game Developer piece or the ReferralCandy teardown, could not open to confirm which] — [Game Developer "The recipe behind Cookie Clicker"](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/the-recipe-behind-cookie-clicker); [ReferralCandy teardown](https://referralcandy.com/blog/cookie-clicker); [TV Tropes](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/VideoGame/CookieClicker)
- **A Dark Room uses motion sparingly but does use it [read].** Combat hits are a 200 ms horizontal jiggle of a text label (`translate(-10px)` → `translate(10px)`, i.e. a CSS-only "shake" on one element rather than the screen); log lines fade in over 500 ms; panels slide 300 ms per panel of distance. — [adarkroom main.css](https://github.com/doublespeakgames/adarkroom/blob/master/css/main.css); [notifications.js](https://github.com/doublespeakgames/adarkroom/blob/master/script/notifications.js); [engine.js](https://github.com/doublespeakgames/adarkroom/blob/master/script/engine.js)
- **Antimatter Dimensions makes every large effect individually switchable [read].** Its default options hold an `animations` block with separate toggles for bigCrunch, eternity, dilation, tachyonParticles, reality and background, and the news ticker has enabled / speed / includeAnimated settings. — [AD player.js](https://github.com/IvarK/AntimatterDimensionsSourceCode/blob/master/src/core/player.js)
- **Nodebuster / Digseum (2024 "short incremental" wave):** Nodebuster (Goblobin, 13 Aug 2024) is 97% positive on ~9.7k Steam reviews; players praise the "satisfying destruction loop" and upgrade progression, and complain about a grindy final stretch. Digseum (Rat Monthly, 9 Dec 2024, ~2.5 h) is described by a forum user as having "a very similar UI and game feel" to Nodebuster. [extract] — [Steam: Nodebuster](https://store.steampowered.com/app/3107330/Nodebuster/); [Steam: Digseum](https://store.steampowered.com/app/3361470/Digseum/); [ResetEra "Bite-sized incremental games"](https://www.resetera.com/threads/bite-sized-incremental-games.1087230/); [Mancunion review, May 2025](https://mancunion.com/2025/05/08/nodebuster-review-a-short-and-sweet-incremental-game)
- Player feedback on small web incrementals asks for exactly the cheap things: "make the buttons jump, stretch and compress when clicked, plus more sound effects"; another jam dev reports their juice pass "was mostly sounds". [extract; individual itch.io comments] — [itch.io post](https://itch.io/post/16797593); [itch.io post](https://itch.io/post/13700155)
- A general idle-design write-up: every small action should give instant gratification "through sound, animation, or a number boost". [extract] — [Tindalos Games on Medium](https://medium.com/tindalos-games/idle-vs-incremental-vs-tycoon-understanding-the-core-mechanics-f12d62f4b9f7)
- **Haptics on the web are Android-only in practice.** `navigator.vibrate()` needs sticky user activation and silently does nothing without hardware [read]; the W3C implementation report lists WebKit (all iOS browsers) as "Not implemented", verified 9 April 2026, and Apple has announced no plans [extract]. Workaround libraries trigger the native tick of a hidden `<input type="checkbox" switch>` on iOS 18+ [extract]. — [MDN Navigator.vibrate](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Navigator/vibrate); [W3C Vibration implementation report](https://w3c.github.io/vibration/reports/implementation.html); [web-haptics browser support](https://www.mintlify.com/lochie/web-haptics/advanced/browser-support); [Software Mansion on web haptics](https://swmansion.com/blog/haptic-feedback-on-the-web-why-the-web-deliberately-refuses-to-be-as-tactile-as-native-apps)

### Inferences
- Mapping the Juicy Breakout headers onto a DOM text game gives a concrete, cheap checklist: (1) *tween-in* every new row/pane/log line (opacity + small translate, 150–700 ms); (2) *squash/stretch* the pressed button with `transform: scale()` on `:active` and a short overshoot on release; (3) *sound* per event class, not one generic click; (4) *particles* as floating "+N" text nodes or glyph bursts animated with transform/opacity and removed on `animationend`; (5) *shake* confined to one element (as A Dark Room does) rather than the viewport; (6) *freeze* as a 50–150 ms pause of tickers at a big milestone; (7) *personality* via a reacting mascot/cursor/prompt; (8) a palette shift as the "finish him" moment.
- Shake belongs to rare, heavy events (crits, prestige, a build "exploding"), not to every tap: in an idle game taps happen hundreds of times per minute, so the "scale to importance" rule from action games implies near-zero shake for the base click.
- Because haptics do not work in iOS Safari, feel on phones must come from visuals and sound; treat vibration as progressive enhancement behind a setting.
- Effects should be designed as separately toggleable layers from day one (AD's model); it also gives a natural hook for `prefers-reduced-motion`.

Only store pages and player reviews surfaced. Tower Wizard appeared only in a chart listing (release 19 June 2025).
- The full 30-step list from "The art of screenshake" was not retrievable. From memory (unverified this session) it also includes muzzle flash, faster bullets, less accuracy, impact effects, enemy knockback, camera lerp, player knockback/gun kick, "sleep" (hit-stop), more bass in the sound, and bigger explosions.
- Steve Swink's "Game Feel" (2008) could not be consulted; no finding is attributed to it.
- No source was found on combo/crit presentation or on sound layering and pitch randomisation for repeated clicks (the search for it did not run). Commonly cited practice (unverified here): vary pitch a few percent per click, rise in pitch during a combo, cap simultaneous voices.
- Cookie Clicker's specific click feedback (floating "+N" numbers, falling cookie particles, wobble, the options that disable them) is well known but was not verified against a source this session.

## 2. Visualising growth: how do good incrementals show the world growing, and why is a visible simulation praised?

### Takeaway
The best-loved incrementals make the number legible as a place: either the screen fills with units you bought (Gnorp, Egg Inc, Cookie Clicker), or the interface itself grows from one button into a multi-pane system (A Dark Room, Candy Box, Paperclips). Developers and critics both say the simple art earns its effect from quantity and from being an honest picture of the underlying numbers.

### Cited Findings
- **(the) Gnorp Apologue.** Developer Myco: "what I wanted to do with (the) Gnorp Apologue was to create a more visual incremental game that was still an incremental game"; he calls the art "programmer art that strove to take it up a notch" and says it was "difficult to come up with non-bloating, compelling visual design for new units". The article is titled "You have to hit the rock: How (the) Gnorp Apologue uses minimal design to maximum effect" and frames the simple art as gaining weight from the sheer number of objects on screen. [extract] — [Game Developer interview](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/interview-the-gnorp-apologue)
- Myco calls the gnorps themselves the biggest hook, describes on-screen simulation detail such as a garden where flowers are grown and fed into a machine, and argues that *visual* incrementals (not idle games in general) are the undersupplied category on Steam. GameDiscoverCo ranks the game the #4 new Steam release of December 2023 by copies sold at $7 and credits YouTube videos and resulting Steam discovery for nearly all of its reach (i.e. it is fun to watch). [extract] — [GameDiscoverCo newsletter](https://newsletter.gamediscover.co/p/how-this-solo-dev-incremental-game)
- The press kit lists as a feature "visuals that accurately portray the nature of the incremental genre"; the developer reports 100k+ copies sold; Steam shows Overwhelmingly Positive on ~7.3k reviews, with reviewers praising "visuals to match the numbers" and animations that are enjoyable to watch. [extract] — [gnorp.dev presskit](https://gnorp.dev/presskit/); [gnorp.dev "100k Players"](https://gnorp.dev/news/11-100k-Players/); [Steam page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1473350); [IncrementalDB entry](https://www.incrementaldb.com/game/the-gnorp-apologue)
- **Egg, Inc.** Gamezebo calls the visuals "fairly plain" yet says watching a small egg farm grow steadily is remarkably satisfying; the same review calls the menu-heavy upgrade decisions "a spreadsheet of sorts". [extract] — [Gamezebo review](https://www.gamezebo.com/reviews/egg-inc-review-eggs-cellent-fun/)
- **Unfolding as growth.** Anthony Pecorella's GDC deck defines a game whose scope changes substantially as an "unfolding game" (examples: A Dark Room, Candy Box, Frog Fractions); his "Math of Idle Games, Part III" tells designers to decide where the "fun" is (new features unfolding, achievements, or optimising the prestige loop) and focus on it. [extract; talks dated 2016, article 2017] — [GDC Vault deck](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/mediaProxy.php?sid=1023876); [Game Developer: The Math of Idle Games, Part III](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/the-math-of-idle-games-part-iii)
- **A Dark Room.** The game starts with a title and a couple of lines of text and ends with the player managing dozens of interdependent resources and a hostile map "using that same interface"; it keeps few items on screen and adds elements slowly so they can be learned. Critics describe the world as building outward from the fire: fire, builder, village, economy, landscape. [extract] — [InformIT "Interface Lessons from A Dark Room"](https://informit.com/articles/article.aspx?p=2228802); [mechanicsofmagic.com critical play](https://mechanicsofmagic.com/?p=55924)
- The developers' own design notes: both "agonized over the opening sequence"; after lighting the fire it takes a little over 15 seconds before "a silent forest" opens up in the iOS version, versus 45 seconds in the original web version, because Townsend feared losing players between first engagement and first surprise. [extract of a primary document] — [Amir Rajan design-process PDF](https://s3.us-east-2.amazonaws.com/amirrajan-public/design-process.pdf)
- **Candy Box.** Creator aniwey told PC Gamer the mystery is deliberate ("the surprise aspect" is what they aimed for) and told Kotaku that "Nothing at first, then it grows" is their favourite part; Game Developer wrote that half the fun is discovering what the creator invents next "with such simple visual resources". [extract; 2013] — [PC Gamer interview](https://www.pcgamer.com/candy-box-1/); [Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/candy-box-a-game-thats-simple-sweet-and-strangely-c-492318079); [Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/why-i-candy-box-i-became-more-social-than-social-games-)
- **Universal Paperclips.** Starts as "just text and numbers" with some geometric shapes introduced later; Frank Lantz: "I love the way [incremental games] make abstract mathematical relationships feel palpable, concrete", and he describes the game as intentionally "minimalist in its presentation... a little thought experiment... a little fable". [extract] — [GamesBeat](https://gamesbeat.com/this-clicker-game-lets-you-take-over-the-world-with-paper-clips/); [50 Years of Text Games: 2017](https://if50.substack.com/p/2017-universal-paperclips); [Y Combinator interview](https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/frank-lantz-director-of-nyus-game-center-and-creator-of-universal-paperclips/)
- One essay contrasts the two: "Universal Paperclips turns the world into numbers; A Dark Room turns numbers into a world." [extract; exact source within the result set not confirmed] — [webiano.digital essay](https://webiano.digital/universal-paperclips-is-the-smartest-game-about-a-dumb-goal/)
- Design guides converge on progressive reveal: "when the player can see all the numbers at once, the mystery is gone", show the next 2–3 upgrades rather than all 20; each new layer should recontextualise what came before; grey out advanced tabs until unlocked. A player complaint shows the failure case: "the sense of unfolding seems lacking and all I really felt like I was doing was watching progress bars". [extract; dev blogs and a marketing blog, moderate authority] — [dev.to "I Built 7 Idle Games in 30 Days"](https://dev.to/aguier/i-built-7-idle-games-in-30-days-what-i-learned-about-incremental-design-5d3f); [0n1 Studios atlas](https://0n1studios.com/atlas/builder/idle-games); [Apptrove](https://apptrove.com/how-to-make-an-idle-game/); [itch.io comment](https://itch.io/post/7179426)
- Kittens Game implements unfolding structurally [read]: on reset every tab except "Bonfire" is set invisible, i.e. tabs are earned again. — [kittensgame game.js](https://github.com/nuclear-unicorn/kittensgame/blob/master/game.js)

### Inferences
- A text/terminal game can have a "visible simulation" without art: a pane of live processes, a scrolling build log whose throughput visibly accelerates, a file tree that gains nodes, a row of ASCII workers per owned unit, a status line that gains segments. The sourced principle is that what is drawn must be an honest function of what is owned (Gnorp press kit) and that quantity is what impresses (Game Developer framing).
- Growth should be shown on two axes at once: density inside a pane (more units) and breadth of the interface (new panes/tabs/status segments). A Dark Room and Candy Box show the second axis alone can carry a game.
- The first surprise should arrive fast; A Dark Room's port cut the first unfold from 45 s to about 15 s.
- Myco's "non-bloating" remark is the constraint to design for: each new unit type needs a distinct, compact representation, and old ones must compress (e.g. "x128" badges, a density glyph) rather than scroll off.

### Gaps
- No sourced description was found of Cookie Clicker's building rows, milk level or changing backdrops, nor of Kittens Game's village visual, AdVenture Capitalist or Idle Slayer; these are widely cited examples but unverified here.
- The Gnorp interview answers on unit expressiveness and readability were cut off in the extract.
- No source found on milestone "transformations" of the whole screen (e.g. Paperclips' phase changes, SPACEPLAN's view changes) beyond the one-line descriptions above.

## 3. Number and progress presentation: formatting, rates, affordability, badges, previews, statistics, offline screens

### Takeaway
Leading web incrementals treat number presentation as a configurable subsystem: a notation setting with many choices, fixed digit rules, rate display that can be switched, storage-cap colouring, per-tab notification highlights and a per-resource "while you were away" summary. Guides add progress bars to the next goal, a short horizon of visible upgrades, and an offline screen that leads straight to the next decision.

### Cited Findings
- **Notation as a setting [read].** Antimatter Dimensions ships its notations as a standalone library exporting: Scientific, Engineering, Letters, Standard, Mixed scientific, Mixed engineering, Logarithm, Mixed Logarithm (Sci), plus joke notations (Emoji, Brackets, Infinity, Roman, Dots, Zalgo, Hex, Imperial, Clock, Prime, Bar, Shi, Blobs, Blind, All, Custom). The API is `format(value, places, placesUnder1000)`, i.e. separate precision above and below 1,000; settings cover when a value shows as "Infinite" and when exponents get commas (default from 100,000). — [antimatter-dimensions/notations index.ts](https://github.com/antimatter-dimensions/notations/blob/master/src/index.ts); [README](https://github.com/antimatter-dimensions/notations/blob/master/README.md)
- AD's default notation is "Mixed scientific" (named suffixes while they are readable, scientific after), with `notationDigits: { comma: 5, notation: 9 }` controlling when commas and when notation start. [read] — [AD player.js](https://github.com/IvarK/AntimatterDimensionsSourceCode/blob/master/src/core/player.js)
- break_infinity.js exists because incrementals need numbers beyond 1e308 (up to 1e9e15) and "want to prioritize speed over accuracy"; break_eternity.js goes further. [read] — [break_infinity.js README](https://github.com/Patashu/break_infinity.js)
- Kittens Game defaults to SI notation (`notation: "si"`) and exposes display options: per-second values on by default (vs per tick), force high precision, percentage resource values, percentage consumption values, show non-applicable buttons, and a bulk `batchSize` of 10. [read] — [kittensgame game.js](https://github.com/nuclear-unicorn/kittensgame/blob/master/game.js); [settings.js](https://github.com/nuclear-unicorn/kittensgame/blob/master/js/settings.js)
- Common approaches named in guides: K/M/B/T suffixes, scientific notation, and precision management; a research note for another terminal-style idle game recommends abbreviating past six digits with fixed precision, fixed-width rates, and always printing the "/s" unit. [extract; the second is a secondhand GitHub issue] — [GridInc blog](https://gridinc.co.za/blog/idle-games-best-practices); [daemon-zero issue #330](https://github.com/williamzujkowski/daemon-zero/issues/330)
- Universal Paperclips just uses JavaScript doubles, so values lose precision as they grow and very large ones fall into scientific notation. [extract] — [50 Years of Text Games](https://if50.substack.com/p/2017-universal-paperclips)
- **Stable digits.** `font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums` activates figures of equal width "allowing them to be easily aligned like in tables"; `slashed-zero` is also available. [read] — [MDN font-variant-numeric](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/Reference/Properties/font-variant-numeric)
- **Affordability / cap colouring [read].** Kittens Game's `highlightUnavailable` (default on) turns buttons red "to signify they're capped due to storage limits"; it also offers hideSell and hideDowngrade to remove rarely used controls. A Dark Room greys disabled buttons (border and text #b2b2b2, default cursor) and shows cost in a hover tooltip that is suppressed while disabled or free. — [kittensgame settings.js](https://github.com/nuclear-unicorn/kittensgame/blob/master/js/settings.js); [adarkroom main.css](https://github.com/doublespeakgames/adarkroom/blob/master/css/main.css)
- **Progress inside the button [read].** A Dark Room draws action cooldowns as a bar behind the button label: an absolutely positioned div (background #DDDDDD, z-index −1) whose width animates linearly from 100% to 0% over the cooldown, with remaining cooldown saved in state so a reload does not reset it. — [adarkroom Button.js](https://github.com/doublespeakgames/adarkroom/blob/master/script/Button.js); [main.css](https://github.com/doublespeakgames/adarkroom/blob/master/css/main.css)
- A Dark Room formats income as signed rate per interval ("+N per Ms"). [read] — [adarkroom engine.js](https://github.com/doublespeakgames/adarkroom/blob/master/script/engine.js)
- **Tab notification badges [read].** AD has a TabNotification system: when a condition first becomes true it marks specific tabs/subtabs as highlighted (unless the player is already on them), fires once (tracked in a bitmask), and force-unhides those tabs. The Modding Tree (the engine behind many community incrementals) automatically highlights a layer node when an upgrade there is affordable, and offers `shouldNotify`, a `glowColor` (default red, "if you want several different notification types"), `prestigeNotify` for a subtler "worth resetting" highlight, and a `marked` star on nodes. — [AD tab-notifications.js](https://github.com/IvarK/AntimatterDimensionsSourceCode/blob/master/src/core/tab-notifications.js); [TMT layer-features.md](https://github.com/Acamaeda/The-Modding-Tree/blob/master/docs/layer-features.md)
- **Prestige-gain preview [read].** TMT's prestige button text is a first-class feature (`resetDescription` replaces "Reset for ", `prestigeButtonText()` builds the whole label), and `canBuyMax()` governs bulk prestige. — [TMT layer-features.md](https://github.com/Acamaeda/The-Modding-Tree/blob/master/docs/layer-features.md)
- Prestige timing rule of thumb from Pecorella's deck: reset when you would gain roughly 50%–200% more prestige currency. [extract; 2016] — [GDC Vault deck](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/mediaProxy.php?sid=1023876)
- **Offline / away screens.** AD stores a per-resource `awayProgress` map (antimatter, dimension boosts, galaxies, infinity points, and so on, each toggleable and only listed once unlocked), plus options for offline ticks (default 1e5) and disabling offline progress [read]. TMT has an `offlineLimit` in hours, with the author noting most such games are fast enough that too much offline time "ruins the balance" [read]. — [AD player.js](https://github.com/IvarK/AntimatterDimensionsSourceCode/blob/master/src/core/player.js); [AD away-progress-types.js](https://github.com/IvarK/AntimatterDimensionsSourceCode/blob/master/src/core/secret-formula/away-progress-types.js); [TMT main-mod-info.md](https://github.com/Acamaeda/The-Modding-Tree/blob/master/docs/main-mod-info.md)
- Guides on the welcome-back screen: it is "one of the most satisfying moments in an idle game"; it should present elapsed time as a readable windfall and tie it directly to the next meaningful choice; do not show it for short absences (one studio requires 3 minutes away so app-switching does not raise a reward screen); measure from the last active visit, not last tab load; bound the offline rate so a boost active at exit does not produce a fortune; reported caps range from 2 to 24 hours. [extract; attribution among these three guides is approximate] — [onirealms "How to Design an Idle Game: Formulas From 4 Shipped Games"](https://onirealms.com/guides/how-to-design-an-idle-game/); [gamedesign.gg "The Design of Waiting"](https://gamedesign.gg/articles/idle-and-incremental-game-design/); [dropletidle guide](https://dropletidle.com/pages/blog/guide-to-ambient-idle-game-mechanics/)
- UI guidance for returning players: progress bars, milestones and floating number tickers so they do not face static numbers; a milestone or visible leap every few minutes; a smaller set of clearly named upgrades beats a crowded screen of tiny percentage bonuses. [extract] — [GridInc blog](https://gridinc.co.za/blog/idle-games-best-practices); [dropletidle guide](https://dropletidle.com/pages/blog/guide-to-ambient-idle-game-mechanics/)
- SPACEPLAN: a reviewer notes interface animations direct attention by showing how many more clicks are needed before something new is revealed (a progress-to-next-unlock indicator). [extract; exact review within the result set not confirmed] — [PC Gamer on SPACEPLAN](https://www.pcgamer.com/uk/spaceplan-is-a-short-and-tasty-clicker-game-where-you-shoot-potatoes-into-orbit/)
- Statistics and hints as options [read]: AD has a statistics tab resource selector, a multiplier-breakdown tab, `showHintText` toggles (percentages, achievement unlock states, challenge hints), and "hide completed achievement rows". — [AD player.js](https://github.com/IvarK/AntimatterDimensionsSourceCode/blob/master/src/core/player.js)

### Inferences
- A sensible default stack for a web idle game: suffix notation up to a readable limit then scientific ("mixed scientific"), 3 significant figures above 1,000, tabular figures, always-visible "/s", and a notation switch in settings. Engineering notation is the natural extra for a programmer-themed audience.
- The in-button fill generalises: the same absolutely positioned bar can show "progress to affordable" (current / cost) on every purchase row, which answers "what should I buy next" without an extra column; pair it with a text "time until affordable" (cost − have) / rate.
- Notification highlights should be one-shot and cleared by visiting the tab (AD's design); permanent glows on every affordable thing become noise. Two tiers (new content vs affordable) match TMT's glowColor idea.
- Offline summaries should be per-resource deltas, suppressed below a few minutes, and end with a call to action.

### Gaps
- No source was found that specifically discusses "time until affordable" displays, buy-max / x10 / x100 selector design, or long-press bulk buying (the search for it did not run). Kittens Game's batch size of 10 and TMT's canBuyMax are the only verified data points.
- No sourced discussion of charts/graphs in incrementals was found.
- The CHI 2018 claim that many idle games fail through incomprehensible interfaces is reported secondhand in the daemon-zero issue and was not traced to the paper.

## 4. Text-first and minimalist aesthetics: how do text/ASCII/terminal-styled games create atmosphere with little art?

### Takeaway
Text-first games succeed when minimalism is treated as the design, not the budget: precise sparse copy, slow reveal, a handful of tuned transitions, and a strict semantic palette. The terminal look (glow, scanlines, flicker) is cheap in CSS, but scanlines and flicker are exactly the patterns photosensitivity guidance warns about, so they need to be subtle and optional.

### Cited Findings
- InformIT's review of A Dark Room argues its interactions are "precise, guided, and simple", that minimalism "is not just a side effect of lazy programming but a valid destination in and of its own", and that a text-based interface can be as successful as a fully graphic one when done carefully. [extract] — [InformIT](https://informit.com/articles/article.aspx?p=2228802)
- A Dark Room's writing uses simple, clipped lowercase sentences to reinforce an unsettling tone ("the fire is dead", "the room is freezing", then one "light fire" button). A critical essay notes the cost: the minimalist interface "risks breaking immersion through boredom and lack of stimulation". [extract] — [Amir Rajan design-process PDF](https://s3.us-east-2.amazonaws.com/amirrajan-public/design-process.pdf); [mechanicsofmagic.com](https://mechanicsofmagic.com/?p=55924)
- **How A Dark Room is actually built [read].** Body font is Times New Roman (serif, not monospace); new log lines are prepended at the top and fade from opacity 0 to 1 over 500 ms; a gradient overlay fades old lines out and lines scrolled past it are removed from the DOM ("to fix some memory usage issues"); a "saved" notice fades out over 1 s; there is a "lights off" (dark mode) toggle and a "hyper" mode that doubles game speed behind a confirmation dialog; store panels slide with the main panel. — [notifications.js](https://github.com/doublespeakgames/adarkroom/blob/master/script/notifications.js); [engine.js](https://github.com/doublespeakgames/adarkroom/blob/master/script/engine.js); [main.css](https://github.com/doublespeakgames/adarkroom/blob/master/css/main.css)
- Universal Paperclips: one critic argues "the lack of spectacle is the spectacle" and that the plain interface is a large part of the design. [extract; exact source within result set not confirmed] — [webiano.digital essay](https://webiano.digital/universal-paperclips-is-the-smartest-game-about-a-dumb-goal/)
- Candy Box uses ASCII art for all visuals and is built on small discoveries. [extract] — [Wikiwand: Candy Box!](https://www.wikiwand.com/en/articles/Candy_Box!)
- **SPACEPLAN.** Jake Hollands wanted a game that looks good using only graphic-design tools (typography, UI, geometric shapes), calling that a scary task that the clicker genre suits; he later "hated how it looked" (visual hierarchy and typography) and remastered it. Inspirations were A Dark Room and Cookie Clicker; it runs about 5–6 hours by design; the soundtrack (Logan Gabriel) is a headline selling point. [extract] — [TechRadar](https://techradar.com/gaming/spaceplans-dev-hated-how-it-looked-so-decided-to-remaster-the-entire-game); [IGF entry](https://igf.com/spaceplan); [Wikipedia: Spaceplan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spaceplan)
- **Bitburner.** Presented as a Linux-like terminal with "simple ASCII-art style" graphics and effectively no sound; a review calls the UI "basic but fairly clear, although there are a few bits of awkwardness", and an aggregated review summary lists poor visual/UI design as a deterrent for some players despite Overwhelmingly Positive reviews (~7.4k). [extract] — [gamebrain.co](https://gamebrain.co/game/bitburner); [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/bitburner-is-an-idle-game-about-hacking-that-teaches-real-javascript); [Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1812820/Bitburner/)
- **Bitburner's palette is semantic and themeable [read].** Its theme interface defines primary / secondary / success / error / warning / info, each in light, normal and dark; dedicated colours per stat (hp, money, hack, combat, cha, int, rep); plus disabled, two background levels, "well", and button. It ships 17 predefined themes, mostly code-editor palettes: Default, Default Lite, Monokai-ish, Warmer, Dark+, Mayukai Dark, Purple, Smooth Green, Dracula, Dark Blue, Discord-like, One Dark, Muted Gold Blue, Light, Iron Man, Zerenity, Catppuccin Mocha. — [bitburner-src Themes.ts](https://github.com/bitburner-official/bitburner-src/blob/dev/src/Themes/Themes.ts); [themes data index](https://github.com/bitburner-official/bitburner-src/blob/dev/src/Themes/data/index.ts)
- **Stimulation Clicker (Neal Agarwal, web, released 6 January 2025)** is the opposite pole: a Cookie-Clicker-style game intended to capture feeling overwhelmed by constant stimulation; coverage called it a clicker "for the TikTok generation". Progress cannot be saved. [extract] — [Wikipedia: Stimulation Clicker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stimulation_Clicker)
- **CRT/terminal effects in CSS.** Glow is stacked `text-shadow` layers of increasing blur; scanlines are typically a `::before`/`::after` overlay; flicker is a `@keyframes` animation on opacity or on a double (e.g. green/red) text-shadow, and "the same effect also works, in a more subdued way, without animation". A retro-design guide advises reduced-motion alternatives for animated scanlines and avoiding heavy flicker. [extract] — [CSS-Tricks](https://css-tricks.com/?p=275830); [dev.to "Retro CRT terminal screen in CSS + JS"](https://dev.to/ekeijl/retro-crt-terminal-screen-in-css-js-4afh); [CodeMyUI flicker example](https://codemyui.com/crt-screen-text-flicker-animation-in-pure-css/); [Retrofuturism design playground](https://drjulsgilliam.github.io/HTML-CSS-Visual-Playground/retrofuturism-design.html)
- **Stripes are a known trigger.** MDN's seizure guidance quotes the Epilepsy Foundation working group: a pattern with potential for provoking seizures "contains clearly discernible stripes, numbering more than five light-dark pairs of stripes in any orientation", and static stripes "have the same effect as flashing lights because of the alternation of dark and bright areas". [read] — [MDN: Web accessibility for seizures and physical reactions](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/Accessibility/Guides/Seizure_disorders)
- Xbox accessibility guidance: if stylistic fonts are used, provide a non-stylised font option. [extract] — [Xbox Accessibility Guidelines](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/gaming/accessibility/guidelines)

### Inferences
- Atmosphere in a text game comes from four cheap levers, all evidenced above: voice (clipped, consistent, lowercase or log-style copy), timing (a fade-in per line; the first reveal within about 15 s), restraint (few elements, added slowly), and a semantic palette (one colour per meaning, as in Bitburner's tokens).
- A code-editor theme set is itself content: Bitburner ships recognisable editor palettes, so syntax-highlight themes (and unlockable ones) are a natural cosmetic reward in a programmer-themed game. Build on CSS custom properties with semantic names (success/warn/error/info/money/disabled) rather than raw hues.
- Scanlines should be very low contrast (a few percent alpha), static rather than rolling, and switchable; high-contrast full-screen stripes conflict with the "more than five light-dark pairs" guidance. Flicker should be off by default or removed under `prefers-reduced-motion`.
- Glow via text-shadow is best reserved for a few emphasised tokens (cursor, milestone numbers): large blurred shadows on text that changes every frame force repaints, which matters on low-end phones (see section 6).
- A log that prepends, fades and prunes (A Dark Room) is a proven pattern for an endless terminal feed; prune offscreen nodes.

### Gaps
- No sourced guidance was found on typewriter text (speed, skippability) or cursor animation; common practice (unverified here) is to make typing instant on tap and disable it under reduced motion.
- No designer commentary from Kittens Game, Bitburner or roguelike ASCII UI authors (e.g. Cogmind's Josh Ge, whose interview appeared in results but was not opened) was retrieved.
- No measurements of the rendering cost of text-shadow glow or overlay scanlines on mobile were found.
- Agarwal's own design commentary on Stimulation Clicker was not found.

## 5. Mobile idle UI conventions: tab bars, thumb zones, one-handed play, density, safe areas

### Takeaway
General mobile UX guidance (bottom-third primary actions, a 3–5 item bottom bar, adequate tap targets, safe-area padding) applies directly to portrait idle games, and web incrementals that added mobile support expose layout choices as settings. I found little genre-specific published guidance on mobile idle layouts, so much of this section rests on general sources plus two games' source code.

### Cited Findings
- The thumb zone is the part of a touchscreen comfortably reachable one-handed; a common split is easy zone (bottom third, primary actions), stretch zone (middle, secondary navigation) and hard zone (top). [extract] — [Smashing Magazine "The Thumb Zone" (2016)](https://www.smashingmagazine.com/2016/09/the-thumb-zone-designing-for-mobile-users); [emergent.sh glossary](https://emergent.sh/glossary/thumb-zone)
- Bottom navigation is recommended for one-handed use, with 3–5 destinations, each an icon plus label sized to accessible tap targets; caveats: crowding the thumb zone causes fat-finger errors, and system gestures (home swipe) conflict with app-level bottom navigation. [extract] — [Smashing Magazine "How To Design Mobile Apps For One-Hand Usage" (2020)](https://smashingmagazine.com/2020/02/design-mobile-apps-one-hand-usage/); [dev.to "Designing for reachability... in 2026"](https://dev.to/del_rosario/designing-for-reachability-interfaces-for-7-inch-phones-in-2026-2b93)
- **Tap target sizes [read].** WCAG 2.2 SC 2.5.8 (AA) requires targets of at least 24 by 24 CSS pixels, or undersized targets spaced so 24 px circles centred on them do not intersect; SC 2.5.5 (AAA) uses 44 by 44 CSS pixels. — [W3C Understanding 2.5.8 Target Size (Minimum)](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/target-size-minimum.html)
- **Safe areas [read].** `env(safe-area-inset-*)` exists so content is "not obscured by device notches or rounded corners"; MDN's example pads a sticky bottom toolbar with `padding: 1em 1em calc(1em + env(safe-area-inset-bottom))`; on desktop the inset is 0. — [MDN env()](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/Reference/Values/env)
- **Kittens Game's mobile options [read].** Settings are registered with separate mobile titles so only relevant ones appear on mobile; mobile-exposed options include swipe navigation between tabs (off by default), a legacy two-in-a-row button layout toggle, number precision, percentage resource values, cap highlighting, hide-sell, and hiding a whole subsystem's UI (pollution); "tooltips in right column" exists as an alternative to hover tooltips. — [kittensgame settings.js](https://github.com/nuclear-unicorn/kittensgame/blob/master/js/settings.js)
- Players of tab-heavy web incrementals ask for a sidebar/quick-access navigation instead of dozens of tabs, and for a clearly distinct active-tab style. [extract] — [galaxy.click comments on Celestial Incremental](https://galaxy.click/comments/470?sort=top); [itch.io comment](https://itch.io/post/11641548)
- AD offers a single "sidebar resource" selector, tab hiding bitmasks and automatic tab switching on key events [read]; TMT offers "force one tab" (single-pane layout) and "force tooltips" (pin tooltips, since hover does not exist on touch) [read]. — [AD player.js](https://github.com/IvarK/AntimatterDimensionsSourceCode/blob/master/src/core/player.js); [TMT options.js](https://github.com/Acamaeda/The-Modding-Tree/blob/master/js/utils/options.js)
- A marketing blog reports (single unverified case study) that moving the prestige button to a central animated badge once the player qualified raised prestige engagement by over 28%. [extract, low-authority] — [Apptrove](https://apptrove.com/how-to-make-an-idle-game/)

### Inferences
- For a portrait web idle game: persistent resource header at top (read-only, so the hard-to-reach zone is fine), the main action and purchase list in the lower two-thirds, a 3–5 item bottom tab bar padded with `env(safe-area-inset-bottom)`, and purchase rows at least 44 px tall.
- Hover tooltips must have a touch equivalent (pinned tooltip pane, tap-to-expand row, or long-press); both Kittens Game and TMT added a setting for this.
- Swipe between tabs is worth offering but as an option, since it conflicts with horizontal scrolling and system back gestures (Kittens ships it off by default).
- Density should be a setting (compact vs comfortable rows); the genre's players skew toward wanting more information per screen, while phones need larger targets.

### Gaps
- No teardown of the portrait layouts of Egg Inc, AdVenture Capitalist, Idle Slayer or other top mobile idle games was retrieved; the usual description (resource header, scene in the upper half, purchase list plus bottom tab bar, x1/x10/Max selector, hold-to-repeat buying) is from memory and unverified here.
- No source on long-press/hold-to-buy, modal-vs-inline purchasing, or bulk-buy selector placement was found (the search did not run).
- The claim that the classic thumb-zone model is outdated for large phones appeared only as a headline fragment ("avoid the Thumb Zone in 2020") and was not examined.

## 6. Themes, accessibility and comfort: reduced motion, flashing, colour, contrast, font size, performance

### Takeaway
The hard limits are clear and easy to respect in CSS: nothing may flash more than three times a second, interaction-triggered motion should be disableable (the `prefers-reduced-motion` query is the sanctioned technique), text needs 4.5:1 contrast, and colour must never be the only signal. The best web incrementals go further with per-effect toggles, multiple themes including true-black, notation choice and a configurable UI update rate.

### Cited Findings
- **Flashing [read / extract].** WCAG SC 2.3.1 (Level A): pages must not contain anything that flashes more than three times in any one-second period unless below the general and red flash thresholds. A general flash is a pair of opposing relative-luminance changes of 10% or more of maximum where the darker image is below 0.80; a red flash is any pair of opposing transitions involving a saturated red. The Epilepsy Foundation consensus: a flash is a potential hazard at luminance ≥20 cd/m², ≥3 Hz, and covering about 25% of screen area at typical viewing distance; 5–30 Hz is the most provocative band. — [W3C Understanding 2.3.1](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/three-flashes-or-below-threshold.html); [MDN seizure guidance](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/Accessibility/Guides/Seizure_disorders)
- Xbox Accessibility Guideline 118: all games should be tested for photosensitive triggers whether or not they contain intentional flashing; mitigations are reducing flash contrast, frequency and size. [extract] — [XAG 118](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-ie/gaming/accessibility/xbox-accessibility-guidelines/118)
- **Motion [read].** WCAG SC 2.3.3 (AAA): motion animation triggered by interaction can be disabled unless essential. The Understanding document's options: avoid unnecessary animation; provide a control to turn off non-essential animation; or use the OS/user-agent reduce-motion feature. Vestibular reactions include dizziness, nausea and headaches; MDN adds that "scaling or panning large objects can be vestibular motion triggers". `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` reflects an OS-level setting. — [W3C Understanding 2.3.3](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG21/Understanding/animation-from-interactions); [MDN prefers-reduced-motion](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/Reference/At-rules/@media/prefers-reduced-motion)
- Game-specific checklists ask: can screen shake and motion blur be turned off? [extract] — [Games for Change accessibility tips PDF](https://gamesforchange.org/studentchallenge/wp-content/uploads/2022/01/Tips_Resources_for-Considering-the-Needs-of-Disabled-Gamers-in-Design.pdf)
- **Contrast and colour.** Standard text needs at least 4.5:1 and large text 3:1; a high-contrast mode should aim for 7:1 [extract]. WCAG treats 4.5:1 and 3:1 as thresholds (4.499:1 fails) [read]. For "use of colour", a second cue such as a lightness difference of at least 3:1 counts as an additional distinction (light green vs dark red passes if contrast between them is ≥3:1) [read]. Colour-only signals are listed among common game barriers [extract]. — [Unity accessibility basics](https://unity.com/blog/video-game-accessibility-basics); [W3C Understanding 1.4.3](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/contrast-minimum.html); [W3C Understanding 1.4.1](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/use-of-color.html)
- Game Accessibility Guidelines (basic tier): use an easily readable default font size, allow font size to be adjusted, and offer a choice of text colour. [extract] — [gameaccessibilityguidelines.com basic](https://gameaccessibilityguidelines.com/basic/)
- **What leading web incrementals ship [read].** Antimatter Dimensions: themes Normal, Metro, Dark, Dark Metro, Inverted, Inverted Metro, AMOLED, AMOLED Metro plus 12 secret unlockable themes (some animated); a classic-vs-modern UI switch (`newUI`); `updateRate: 33` ms as a user setting; per-layer animation toggles; per-reset confirmation toggles (about 20); glyph colour options including `highContrastRarity` and swap-colours; hotkeys. The Modding Tree: theme, "high-quality tree" off by default, hide milestone popups, hide completed challenges, old style. Kittens Game: hide background image; hide a subsystem's UI. A Dark Room: lights-off mode and 2x "hyper" speed. — [AD themes.js](https://github.com/IvarK/AntimatterDimensionsSourceCode/blob/master/src/core/themes.js); [AD player.js](https://github.com/IvarK/AntimatterDimensionsSourceCode/blob/master/src/core/player.js); [TMT options.js](https://github.com/Acamaeda/The-Modding-Tree/blob/master/js/utils/options.js); [kittensgame settings.js](https://github.com/nuclear-unicorn/kittensgame/blob/master/js/settings.js); [adarkroom engine.js](https://github.com/doublespeakgames/adarkroom/blob/master/script/engine.js)
- **Performance.** `transform` and `opacity` (also `filter`, `clip-path`) can be animated on the compositor, bypassing layout and paint; layout thrashing is interleaving DOM writes with geometry reads such as `getBoundingClientRect()`; batch writes in `requestAnimationFrame`; the transform/opacity preference is a guideline to verify by measurement. [extract] — [web.dev animations overview](https://web.dev/articles/animations-overview.md.txt); [Motion: web animation performance tier list](https://motion.dev/magazine/web-animation-performance-tier-list); [dev.to on layout thrashing](https://dev.to/gokul369/layout-thrashing-the-hidden-performance-killer-in-modern-web-apps-51me)
- `content-visibility: auto` lets the browser skip layout and painting of off-screen content while keeping it in the DOM, accessibility tree and find-in-page. [read] — [MDN content-visibility](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/Reference/Properties/content-visibility)
- Long-running text games prune: A Dark Room removes hidden log lines each time a new one is added "to fix some memory usage issues... this way we never have a large backlog to iterate through". [read] — [adarkroom notifications.js](https://github.com/doublespeakgames/adarkroom/blob/master/script/notifications.js)

### Inferences
- Minimum comfort set for a web idle game: honour `prefers-reduced-motion` automatically and also offer an in-game "reduce motion" switch (WCAG lists both routes); separate toggles for floating numbers, shake, flashes/glow, scanlines and background animation; a full-screen flash must never repeat more than three times a second, and bright full-screen flashes on prestige should be a single slow fade.
- State colours should be double-coded: affordable vs not = colour plus weight/opacity/glyph (e.g. a leading `+`/`·`/`!`), and red/green pairs should differ in lightness by at least 3:1 so they survive red-green colour blindness.
- Offer a true-black (AMOLED) theme and at least one light theme; AD and Bitburner both treat theme count as a feature.
- Decouple simulation tick from DOM writes: update text nodes at a capped rate (AD's default is about 30 Hz and is user-adjustable; 4–10 Hz is plausible for large numbers), write only when the formatted string changes, animate only transform/opacity, and use `content-visibility: auto` on long upgrade lists and logs.

### Gaps
- No benchmark data was found for DOM text updates per second on low-end phones, nor for `textContent` vs other update methods.
- No colour-blind-safe palette recommendations specific to dark terminal themes were found; no data on what share of incremental players change notation or theme.
- The Game Accessibility Guidelines and Xbox guideline pages were only seen as extracts.

## 7. Reward moments: how are prestige, big unlocks, achievements and milestones staged, and what makes prestige feel good rather than like a loss?

### Takeaway
Prestige feels good when the player sees the gain before committing, the reset itself is a brief staged event, and the next run is visibly faster within seconds. Smaller moments (milestones, achievements, new tabs) are staged as popups and highlights that veterans can switch off.

### Cited Findings
- **A staged reset in code [read].** Antimatter Dimensions' first prestige ("Big Crunch") plays a full-screen CSS animation named `a-implode` on `document.body` for 2 s; the actual reset fires 1 s in, at the midpoint, so the state swap is hidden by the animation; it is skipped if another full-screen animation is already playing or if the player disabled it; a confirmation modal is shown before the first-ever crunch; and on the first crunch the game changes tab to the newly unlocked area. Later layers (eternity, dilation, reality) each have their own animation toggle and confirmation toggle. — [AD big-crunch.js](https://github.com/IvarK/AntimatterDimensionsSourceCode/blob/master/src/core/big-crunch.js); [full-screen-animation-handler.js](https://github.com/IvarK/AntimatterDimensionsSourceCode/blob/master/src/core/full-screen-animation-handler.js); [player.js](https://github.com/IvarK/AntimatterDimensionsSourceCode/blob/master/src/core/player.js)
- Pecorella's "Quest for Progress" (GDC Europe 2016): top player motivators were Completion and Power, and designers should make sure players "feel" their growth; varied prestige systems provide surprises but are harder to design; a common rule is to reset when you would gain 50%–200% more prestige currency. [extract] — [Quest for Progress slides](https://www.slideshare.net/slideshow/quest-for-progress-gdc-europe-2016/65405507); [GDC Vault deck](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/mediaProxy.php?sid=1023876)
- A developer who shipped several small idle games: after a prestige the next run should feel visibly faster within the first 30 seconds, and the first prestige should come quickly so the loop hooks before depth is added. [extract; individual dev blog] — [dev.to "I Built 7 Idle Games in 30 Days"](https://dev.to/aguier/i-built-7-idle-games-in-30-days-what-i-learned-about-incremental-design-5d3f)
- Framing: "starting over doesn't feel like failure; it feels like power"; a square-root reward curve gives big early jumps and diminishing later ones. [extract; individual devlog] — [dev.to economy devlog](https://dev.to/tanishpaul1106/aura-data-and-prestige-designing-the-economy-of-doomscroll-2077-293f)
- The risk: players often find voluntary resets counterintuitive and it "feels like a huge loss"; one studio argues a forced early reset feels unacceptable at first but then makes sessions more interesting. [extract] — [Funovus: Gold, Goblins, and Good Game Design](https://funovus.com/blogs/Gold-and-Goblins)
- Dissent: a genre developer's "10 rules" post calls prestige "a crutch for developers that can't design a proper game". [extract; opinion] — [Subpixel on Patreon](https://www.patreon.com/Subpixel/posts/10-rules-for-160530914)
- **Milestones and achievements [read].** The Modding Tree shows popups for milestones and achievements by default and lets players disable them (`hideMilestonePopups`; per-layer `milestonePopups: false`, `achievementsPopups: false`); a milestone display filter cycles through always / last / automation / incomplete / never; milestones can carry toggles (e.g. automation switches) that appear when earned; achievement tooltips show goal and reward. TMT also has an `isEndgame()` condition that raises a "you win!" screen. — [TMT options.js](https://github.com/Acamaeda/The-Modding-Tree/blob/master/js/utils/options.js); [milestones.md](https://github.com/Acamaeda/The-Modding-Tree/blob/master/docs/milestones.md); [achievements.md](https://github.com/Acamaeda/The-Modding-Tree/blob/master/docs/achievements.md); [main-mod-info.md](https://github.com/Acamaeda/The-Modding-Tree/blob/master/docs/main-mod-info.md)
- Unlocks staged as navigation: AD highlights and force-unhides the relevant tabs once when a condition is first met. [read] — [AD tab-notifications.js](https://github.com/IvarK/AntimatterDimensionsSourceCode/blob/master/src/core/tab-notifications.js)
- Short, finite arcs are themselves a reward structure in the 2024–2026 wave: incrementals are described as "short-but-sweet experiences" priced below a cup of coffee; Nodebuster is a few-hour game; SPACEPLAN was deliberately 5–6 hours because its developer dislikes endless clickers. [extract] — [Phenixx Gaming, Aug 2025](https://web.phenixxgaming.com/2025/08/18/the-delight-of-incremental-gaming/); [Steam: Nodebuster](https://store.steampowered.com/app/3107330/Nodebuster/); [Wikipedia: Spaceplan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spaceplan)

### Inferences
- A prestige sequence that follows the evidence: (1) persistent preview of the gain on the button itself; (2) confirmation the first time only, with a plain list of what is kept, lost and gained; (3) a 1–2 s full-screen transition with the state swap at the midpoint (AD's pattern), replaced by a simple cross-fade under reduced motion; (4) land the player on the new or changed pane with a one-shot highlight; (5) make the first 30 seconds visibly faster, e.g. early purchases that auto-complete or a visibly larger rate figure; (6) let veterans disable the animation and the confirmation separately.
- A run-summary card at the reset moment (time taken, peak rate, best-ever comparison) would turn "loss" into "score"; this is a reasonable extension of Pecorella's "feel their growth" point, but I found no source that tests it.
- Milestone popups should be queued toasts (never modal) and filterable; they double as the place to hand over automation toggles.

### Gaps
- No sourced description of how Cookie Clicker's ascension, Egg Inc's prestige, Kittens Game's reset or Universal Paperclips' phase transitions are staged (the search for it did not run). The widely described Cookie Clicker ascension screen (a separate upgrade tree shown before reincarnating) is unverified here.
- No source on sound stingers, slow-motion or summary screens at prestige in incrementals was found.
- The "+28% prestige engagement from an animated central badge" figure is a single unverified marketing claim.

## 8. Common visual/UX complaints from players of incrementals, and how successful games addressed them

### Takeaway
The recurring complaints are about legibility rather than the core loop: too many tabs with no cue for which matters, unclear cause and effect, spreadsheet-like walls of upgrades, progress that hides when you leave a tab, and plain visuals that put newcomers off. The fixes that recur in successful games are progressive reveal, one-shot tab highlights, hideable and collapsible UI, a short visible upgrade horizon, and a visible scene that mirrors the numbers.

### Inferences
- "What should I do next?" is the single most valuable question for the UI to answer at all times. Evidence-backed mechanisms: a highlighted next-affordable purchase, progress-to-affordable bars, one-shot tab badges, and a short visible horizon of upcoming unlocks (teasers as greyed "???" rows).
- Keep everything progressing regardless of which tab is open, and show a compact global status strip so no system feels hidden.
- Once bought-out, upgrades should collapse into a summary line; AD and Kittens both ship hide-completed options because long-running players ask for them.
- For a terminal-styled game the "ugly spreadsheet" risk is high; the mitigations are a living scene/log pane beside the tables (section 2), a strict type scale with tabular figures, and a restrained semantic palette (section 4).
